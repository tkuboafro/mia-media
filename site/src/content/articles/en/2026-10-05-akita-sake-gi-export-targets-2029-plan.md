## Exports to Rise Roughly 1.5-Fold in Four Years

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/25484685/pexels-photo-25484685.jpeg?auto=compress&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;dpr=2&amp;h=650&amp;w=940" alt="A landscape of ripening rice paddies in Japan" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>A landscape of ripening rice paddies in Japan — Photo: Huu Huynh (Pexels)</figcaption></figure>

Akita Prefecture is a rice-growing region in northern Honshu, Japan, facing the Sea of Japan. It has long been known as a land of sake brewing. In March 2026 the prefecture drew up the "Akita Prefecture Food Industry Promotion Action Plan", which it made public in April. The plan covers the four years from fiscal 2026 to fiscal 2029. The prefecture's new comprehensive plan, which began in the same fiscal year, has a field called "Tourism and Exchange", and this action plan turns the food industry policies within it into concrete measures. It even specifies which projects will be used to carry them out. The most striking figure is the export target. The prefecture aims to raise combined exports of processed foods and sake by roughly 1.5 times, from ¥990 million to ¥1.52 billion. Taiwan, South Korea and Hong Kong are among the main export destinations named.

## Riding the UNESCO Listing, Protecting the Name of the Region with a GI

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/19015375/pexels-photo-19015375.jpeg?auto=compress&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;dpr=2&amp;h=650&amp;w=940" alt="Bottles of sake and brewing barrels lined up in a brewery" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Bottles of sake and brewing barrels lined up in a brewery — Photo: Florian Grewe (Pexels)</figcaption></figure>

Behind the plan lies the inscription of Japan's "traditional sake brewing" on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list. The technique of making sake from rice using koji was recognised internationally. The prefecture sees this as an opportunity to spread the word about Akita's sake. One of the pillars is an effort to obtain Geographical Indication (GI) status for the prefecture's sake. A GI is a system in which the state recognises quality and characteristics tied to a place of origin and protects the name. The idea is close to that of appellations of origin for wine. The GI for "Japanese sake" covering the whole of Japan is already protected within the EU under the economic partnership agreement between Japan and the EU. If a prefecture-level GI is added, "where a sake was made" would carry greater weight on the label. Alongside the GI, the prefecture also intends to build a brand for Akita's sake.

## What This Plan Means from a European Perspective

The export destinations named in the plan are mostly nearby Asian markets. Even so, the move to obtain a GI at the regional level and build a brand is relevant to sake fans in Europe too. In the world of sake, the prefecture or region where a bottle was made is becoming a clue for choosing, much as it is with wine.

If you come across Akita sake in Europe, take a look at the place of origin shown on the label. If an Akita GI is realised in future, that mark would signal both quality and origin. Pour it into a wine glass and compare the aroma when chilled and when closer to room temperature, and you may sense the range of flavour that comes from rice and koji. With the UNESCO listing as a catalyst, how will Japan's regions present their own sake to the world? Akita's four-year plan should serve as one useful gauge of that movement.
