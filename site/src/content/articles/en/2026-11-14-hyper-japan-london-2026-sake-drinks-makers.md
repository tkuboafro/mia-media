From 24 to 26 July 2026, HYPER JAPAN Festival 2026, a large-scale event presenting Japanese pop culture and food, was held in London, UK. According to an on-site report by Nikkei Cross Trend, alongside the manga and game exhibitors, Japanese alcohol and drinks makers such as Suntory, Takara Shuzo and Ito En also had stands. The companies appear to have used the occasion as a chance to put their products directly into the hands of British consumers.

## A gateway to the European market

HYPER JAPAN is designed as a place for Japanese companies and local governments to develop the British and European markets. At the heart of the main hall, stands from companies, local governments and tourism bodies aiming to enter the market are gathered together, and the event's character as a trade fair for ordinary consumers is clear. At the venue, the Japan External Trade Organization (JETRO) set up a stand where visitors could try 30 Japanese indie games. The publisher Shueisha ran a campaign to increase readers of "MANGA Plus", its manga distribution service for overseas audiences.

## Japanese sake spreading as part of the cultural experience

According to the report, promotional activity by drinks makers was lively, including Takara Shuzo's "MIO" and Ito En's green tea drink "Oi Ocha". MIO is a sparkling sake from Takara Shuzo, characterised by its low alcohol content and its sweet, light taste. Visitors' interest was directed not only at manga and anime but at Japanese culture as a whole. The stage events and merchandise areas were also reportedly very busy. It is worth noting that this is becoming a way in for fans of Japanese culture to try sake. The UK is not a member of the EU, but the response in London could also offer clues for expanding sales channels across Europe.

## How to enjoy it in Europe

For anyone who has not yet tried sake, a sparkling sake such as MIO is an easy option to start with. Serve it well chilled in a wine glass or flute, and enjoy it as an aperitif or alongside a fruit-based dessert. To find it, the quickest route is to ask for "sparkling sake" at a Japanese food shop or a sake importer. Drinks makers sometimes also set up tasting stands at Japanese culture events held across Europe. By looking at which products the major makers are putting forward, you can see which Japanese drinks are being promoted in Europe right now.
