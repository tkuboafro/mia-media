## DAITO's 11th year: from art to traditional craft

Suigei Shuzo in Kochi Prefecture (a prefecture in the south of Shikoku, facing the Pacific Ocean) has launched its finest sake, Suigei Junmai Daiginjo DAITO, each year in collaboration with a renowned artist. Now in its 11th year, the brewery explains that it is taking the series beyond the world of sake and developing it into a project that promotes Japanese traditional crafts of which the country can be proud worldwide. The first craft chosen is Tokoname ware (Tokoname-yaki), a style of pottery with a history of more than a thousand years.

This year's partner is Umehara Shoji of Shoryu-gama kiln. He holds the title of "Traditional Craftsman" (Dento Kogeishi), an official Japanese qualification awarded to skilled artisans, and is one of the leading makers of Tokoname ware. Umehara is known for his "Shoryu Tenmoku" pieces, which have a distinctive lustre. Tenmoku is a traditional technique that uses an iron-bearing glaze to create deep colours and a glossy finish, and it has long been used for tea bowls and similar wares.

## The brewery's pinnacle: Yamada Nishiki polished down to 30%

DAITO is made from Yamada Nishiki rice grown in the "Special A district" of Tojo in Hyogo Prefecture. Yamada Nishiki is regarded as one of the finest sake rice varieties, and Tojo is known as its most celebrated growing area. The rice is milled to a polishing ratio of 30%, meaning that the outer 70% is removed and only the heart of the grain is kept, and it is brewed using two types of yeast. "Junmai Daiginjo" is the top grade of sake, brewed without added distilled alcohol and with the rice polished by more than half.

The price is 23,100 yen (tax included) for a 720 ml bottle. A set combining an original Shoryu-gama sake vessel, one katakuchi (a vessel with a pouring spout) and two sake cups, costs 110,000 yen (tax included).

## What European readers may want to note

You can enjoy this story without travelling to Japan. DAITO takes an idea close to the "artist label" of the wine world and extends it to a tangible craft object, the vessel. If you are searching in Europe, ask importers who handle Suigei or specialist sake shops whether they plan to stock DAITO 2026 or the sake vessel set. Once you get hold of a bottle, you might start by serving it lightly chilled and letting it warm up slowly, noting how the aroma evolves. Pour it from the katakuchi and savour the gloss and feel of the cup as well, and sake and craft will become a single experience. It will also be worth watching which craft the brewery teams up with next.
