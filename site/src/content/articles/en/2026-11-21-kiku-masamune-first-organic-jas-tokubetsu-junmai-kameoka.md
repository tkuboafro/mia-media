Kikumasamune Shuzo, a major sake brewery in Hyogo Prefecture, has released its first sake to receive JAS organic certification. It is a Tokubetsu Junmai sake called "Tsuchi to Ta to Organic Terroir of Kameoka". It went on sale in limited quantities on 13 November 2026, with a recommended retail price in Japan of 4,545 yen (excluding tax) for a 720 ml bottle. The fact that a major brewery with such a long history has put out a sake that is organic right down to its raw materials is being watched as a sign of change in the sake industry.

## Made only from organic Yamada Nishiki from Kameoka, Kyoto

The rice used is 100% Yamada Nishiki grown organically in Kameoka, Kyoto Prefecture, from the 2025 harvest. Kameoka is a basin town immediately to the west of the city of Kyoto. Yamada Nishiki is widely known as one of the varieties of rice best suited to sake brewing. JAS organic is a certification system for organic food established by Japan's Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries, and its requirements include cultivation that does not rely on chemically synthesised pesticides or chemical fertilisers.

"Tokubetsu Junmai" is one of the grades of Japanese sake. It is made with only rice, rice koji and water, with no added brewer's alcohol, and is given to sake whose ingredients or production methods involve special care. By narrowing the sake down to a single variety and a single place of origin, this release puts what wine drinkers call "terroir" at the front of its brewing.

## The story of "soil", "paddy" and "village" in the name

The name "Tsuchi to Ta to" reflects the brewery's thinking. "Tsuchi" (soil) stands for nature itself, while "Ta" (paddy) stands for the place that comes into being when people work with that nature. The name carries the hope that the two will continue side by side and eventually lead to a thriving "sato" (village), where people gather. Through the name of a single bottle of sake, the brewery seeks to convey the links between the environment, farming and the local community.

## Enjoying it in Europe

This sake is a limited release in Japan, and for now it is not known whether it will be imported to Europe. Even so, the fact that a major brewery has begun working on organically certified sake is an important development for European readers. In Europe, where many consumers value sustainability, the choice of organic sake is expected to grow. When choosing sake at a specialist shop, look at the label for an organic certification mark and for the rice variety and place of origin. Junmai sake is best served slightly chilled or at room temperature in a wide-mouthed wine glass, which makes the aroma and umami of the rice easier to appreciate. It is recommended to pair it with dishes that bring out the flavour of the ingredients, such as vegetable and mushroom dishes.
