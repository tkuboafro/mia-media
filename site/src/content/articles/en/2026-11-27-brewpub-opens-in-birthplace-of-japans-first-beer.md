Sanda, in Hyogo Prefecture, is an inland city north of Kobe. A new beer company, Hyogo Jozo, has been founded here, and according to the Yomiuri Shimbun it was due to open a brewpub, combining a brewery and a dining area, on 22 November. Sanda is the birthplace of Koumin Kawamoto (1810–1871), the inventor said to have brewed Japan's first beer. At the starting point of Japanese beer history, a new generation of brewers is now getting to work.

## A late Edo-period scholar's written recipe for beer

Koumin Kawamoto was a scholar and inventor who was active from the end of the Edo period into the early Meiji period. The surviving 1861 edition of *Kagaku Shinsho*, a chemistry book he translated, contains a method for making beer. Kawamoto himself is said to have reproduced beer using this method. Sanda holds a "Beer Kentei" (beer quiz) every year, and this history is a source of pride for the town.

## Beer made with sake yeast wins an international gold medal

The brewer is Kenji Okiyoshi (29), who is from Kobe. While working at a human-resources consulting firm in Tokyo, Okiyoshi was influenced by the owner of a brewpub near where he lived. That owner valued "brewing beer that treasures the voices of consumers". In 2021 Okiyoshi returned to his home region and began working at a brewing company in Kobe. The following year he moved to a craft brewery in Osaka, where he learned about blending ingredients and temperature control. There he brewed a beer using sake yeast, which won a gold medal at an international beer competition held in South Korea. The new brewery is a converted former ironworks in the city, which he rented. Equipment such as the fermentation tanks was bought from a craft brewery that is closing down.

## A spicy farmhouse ale and home-grown hops

The brewpub planned to serve three beers at opening. One of them is a dry "farmhouse ale" with a spicy aroma. Beers made with three varieties of hops grown in the field in front of the brewery are also planned for the future. Okiyoshi aims to make beer whose aroma can be enjoyed together with food. His wife, Wakana (30), is in charge of dishes using herbs and pickles made from local vegetables. Okiyoshi cites the German proverb "Drink within sight of the chimney" and says that freshness is the most important thing in beer.

If you live in Europe, you are unlikely ever to have the chance to drink this brewery's beer. Still, there are two trends worth watching. One is the effort of Japanese craft brewers to bring Japanese ingredients, such as sake yeast, into beer. If you spot a Japanese beer on an import shelf with "sake yeast" on the label, try it and see what aromas you find. The other is that the farmhouse ale style, long brewed by farmers in Belgium and France, is now being made in Japan too, tied to local farm produce. In both cases, follow Okiyoshi's words and value freshness: choose bottles with a recent production date, and drink them chilled and soon after buying.
