Foreign capital is taking concrete shape in the Japanese whisky industry. Cedarfield GK (headquartered in Tokyo), a group company of IJW Whiskey, based in Danville, Kentucky, in the United States, is building a distillery in the Chitose Kurinku Industrial Park in Chitose, Hokkaido. The industrial park lies close to New Chitose Airport, Hokkaido's air gateway, and is located south of Sapporo. The plan is to produce 20 million litres a year, which is expected to make it one of the largest facilities in Japan. Real Economy, a Hokkaido business news site, reported on it at the end of August 2026.

## A 50,000-tsubo distillery appears in the industrial park

The site covers about 164,000 square metres (roughly 50,000 tsubo), comparable in size to the sites of Denso Hokkaido, Hokkaido Kikkoman and Mitsumi Electric, which are also located in the same park. Large buildings are already visible on site, and distillation equipment shipped from the United States and elsewhere is being delivered. Construction began in February 2025 and is scheduled to be completed at the end of June 2027. The opening to the public is expected to follow after that.

## Design supervised by Kengo Kuma and an automated maturation warehouse

The basic design and design supervision are being handled by the office of architect Kengo Kuma, known for the National Stadium in Tokyo and other works. Design, site supervision and construction are being carried out by Itogumi Construction of Sapporo. For storing and managing the new-make spirit, the company will install an automated warehouse from Murata Machinery of Kyoto. It is a system for managing a large number of casks over a long period, and it gives a sense of the scale of production.

## What it means to make "Japanese whisky"

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/7599324/pexels-photo-7599324.jpeg?auto=compress&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;dpr=2&amp;h=650&amp;w=940" alt="Impression of a Japanese whisky tasting" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Impression of a Japanese whisky tasting — Photo: Michael  M (Pexels)</figcaption></figure>

The company plans to make Japanese whisky mashed with water from Japan and matured for at least three years. Under the labelling standards of Japan's industry body, calling a product "Japanese whisky" requires, among other things, mashing and distilling in Japan and maturing for at least three years. The rule exists to distinguish such products from those that are merely bottled from spirit imported from overseas. That foreign capital is planning large-scale production to meet this standard from the outset appears to signal a change in how Japanese whisky is supplied.

For European readers, the first whisky from this distillery is still a long way from reaching the market. With completion in mid-2027 and at least three years of maturation, it will be the early 2030s at the very soonest. Until then, it is a good idea to get into the habit of checking the "Japanese Whisky" wording on the label, and whether the whisky was matured in Japan, when choosing a bottle. How spirit matured in Hokkaido's cool climate will taste under US ownership, and how this plan may affect the range and prices of Japanese whisky on European shelves in future, are well worth watching.
