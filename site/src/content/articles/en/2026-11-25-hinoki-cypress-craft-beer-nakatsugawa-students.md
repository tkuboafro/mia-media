Nakatsugawa, in the south-east of Gifu Prefecture in central Japan, is a town surrounded by mountains with a long tradition of forestry. Here, students from Institute of Science Tokyo, a national university in Tokyo, have developed a craft beer called “Yama no Mamoribito” (meaning “guardians of the mountain”), made with extract of locally grown hinoki, a conifer native to Japan known in English as Japanese cypress. According to the Chunichi Shimbun, the beer is on sale at a brewery in the city that helped with its development.

## A town-planning laboratory meets a new brewery

The beer was developed by students in the laboratory of Professor Yosuke Mano, who specialises in town planning. For about ten years the laboratory has used Nakatsugawa as a place for research and practice, carrying out activities to revitalise the area. In the course of this work, they met Kazuki Horikawa, the head of NAKATSUGAWA BREWERY, which opened this summer in the city's Ota-cho district, and the two sides agreed to brew a beer that could only come from Nakatsugawa together. What makes the project interesting is that a university laboratory teamed up with a new, small local brewery to express the character of the town in a single glass of beer.

## Why hinoki?

Hinoki is a wood that has long been treasured in Japan, with a distinctive, clean fragrance. In Japan it has been used for bathtubs and for the “masu”, the square wooden cups from which sake is drunk. By bringing a material that symbolises Nakatsugawa’s main industry, forestry, into beer, the students wanted to convey the presence of the local mountains and the people who look after them, and that idea is behind the name “Yama no Mamoribito”. Europe, too, has a tradition of beers made with woodland ingredients such as spruce tips and juniper, and this beer could be seen as one expression of that idea using a Japanese tree.

## Enjoying it in Europe

“Yama no Mamoribito” is currently sold only at the brewery in Nakatsugawa, and there has been no word of it becoming available in Europe. Even so, the story offers a clue to a new trend in Japanese craft beer. In Japan, beers and sake made with local plants such as hinoki, sugi (Japanese cedar), yuzu and sansho pepper are on the rise. If you come across products labelled “hinoki” or “sugi” in a specialist shop for Japanese sake and beer in Europe, do give them a try. To enjoy the woody aroma, it is best not to over-chill the beer and to pour it into a wide-mouthed glass. You can also drink sake from a hinoki masu and experience at home how the scent of the wood mingles with the sake.
