## Japan's champion, chosen from 215 entries

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/4485375/pexels-photo-4485375.jpeg?auto=compress&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;dpr=2&amp;h=650&amp;w=940" alt="A bartender finishing a cocktail at the bar counter" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>A bartender finishing a cocktail at the bar counter — Photo: Antoni Shkraba (Pexels)</figcaption></figure>

Sanwa Shurui, a sake and spirits producer in Oita Prefecture (in north-eastern Kyushu), is holding the "iichiko Irodori Cocktail Competition 2026", a cocktail contest based on its honkaku barley shochu, "iichiko Saiten". The final judging round of the Japan competition took place in Tokyo on 6 November. Honkaku shochu is a Japanese spirit made by distilling only once an ingredient fermented with koji (grain on which koji mould has been cultivated), and it is known for retaining the flavour of its raw materials. The aim of the competition is to spread the word about new appeals of honkaku shochu through cocktails and to find bartenders who will lead the next generation. A total of 215 people from across the country applied, and seven who made it through the first and second rounds of judging competed in the practical round.

## "Umami Synergy": a drink built on layered umami

The winner was Norihiko Furuse of the bar Zentis Osaka UPSTAIRZ in Osaka. His creation is called "Umami Synergy". He combined the umami that comes from the barley koji used in iichiko Saiten with the umami derived from the rice koji in white miso. In Japanese cooking, several ingredients are combined to make dashi stock and intensify umami. Furuse brought this idea into a cocktail, then added the heat of wasabi and the fresh aroma of oba (shiso leaf) to give it a distinctly Japanese character. According to Furuse, iichiko Saiten gave him a major hint as he considered the question "What is a Japanese cocktail?" He says that engaging seriously with his own experiences and feelings, and being able to put them firmly into his creation, led to the win.

## 2027: representing Japan on the world stage

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/7377095/pexels-photo-7377095.jpeg?auto=compress&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;dpr=2&amp;h=650&amp;w=940" alt="A pale cocktail garnished with green leaves" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>A pale cocktail garnished with green leaves — Photo: Arina Krasnikova (Pexels)</figcaption></figure>

Furuse and the runner-up, Kieko Kawachi (of STARBUCKS RESERVE ROASTERY TOKYO TEAVANA in Tokyo), will represent Japan at the Global Final, scheduled for 2027. Efforts to grow shochu into a brand that is recognised worldwide are now taking shape in the bartending world too.

If you would like to try shochu in Europe, look for barley shochu labelled "honkaku" at Japanese food shops or cocktail bars. Many barley shochus have a toasty, light mouthfeel, and besides being drunk with soda or on the rocks, they can also be used as a cocktail base in place of gin or vodka. The idea behind this winning drink, pairing shochu with ingredients rich in umami or aroma such as miso and herbs, is something you can try at home too. It will also be interesting to see what kind of shochu cocktails appear at the 2027 Global Final.
