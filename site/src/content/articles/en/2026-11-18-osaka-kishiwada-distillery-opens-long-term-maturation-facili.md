A whisky distillery in Kishiwada, Osaka Prefecture, has opened a new facility dedicated to long-term maturation and held a commemorative ceremony. Kishiwada is a town in the south of Osaka Prefecture, facing Osaka Bay. The distillery opened in 2025 and is the second whisky distillery in Osaka Prefecture. The new facility will strengthen its capacity to mature new-make spirit patiently.

## Maturation shapes the quality of whisky

When freshly distilled, whisky is a colourless, rough spirit. Over long years in wooden casks, it develops its colour, aroma and mellowness. For that reason, how much spirit can be matured, for how long, and in what kind of environment has a major influence on the final quality. That a distillery so recently opened has put in place a facility dedicated to long-term maturation at such an early stage can be seen as an investment looking beyond young products that can be shipped straight away, towards serious, time-intensive spirit production.

## New makers with an eye on international markets

Japanese whisky has in recent years earned high praise overseas, including in Europe, and new distilleries are appearing one after another across Japan. For these newcomers, the key to winning trust in international markets is the quality of maturation. That new distilleries are emerging even near a major metropolitan area such as Osaka, and are investing in maturation facilities, suggests that Japan's whisky industry is moving towards raising quality, not merely expanding volume.

## How to enjoy it in Europe

It will still be several years before the long-matured spirit becomes a product. What is worth watching in the meantime are the young releases from Japan's new distilleries. Getting to know the taste of young spirit makes it easier to sense the changes that time brings when you later compare it with long-matured bottlings. If you spot the name of a new Japanese distillery at a specialist shop or whisky fair in Europe, pay attention to the age statement and cask type on the label. You can also check the aroma in a tasting glass and add a little water to see how the aroma opens up.
