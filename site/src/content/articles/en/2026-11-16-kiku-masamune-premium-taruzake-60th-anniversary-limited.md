## A Special Bottle for the 60th Anniversary of Barrel Sake, Limited to 1,000

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/38812748/pexels-photo-38812748.jpeg?auto=compress&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;dpr=2&amp;h=650&amp;w=940" alt="Image of sake packed in a cedar barrel" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Image of sake packed in a cedar barrel — Photo: Muhammed Fatih  KARAKUŞ (Pexels)</figcaption></figure>

Kiku-Masamune Sake Brewing, a sake brewery in Hyogo Prefecture, has released “Kiku-Masamune Premium Taruzake” (720ml) in a limited run of 1,000 bottles to mark the 60th anniversary of its barrel sake. According to the Nihon Shokuryo Shimbun, a Japanese food industry newspaper, it went on sale on 16 November 2026. It is available from only two outlets: the company's official online shop and the Kiku-Masamune Sake Brewing Memorial Museum, which presents the brewery's history. The online shop had been taking reservations since the 10th. The recommended retail price is 3,619 yen before tax. The sake was made as a gift to celebrate important milestones in life, such as new beginnings and anniversaries.

## Three Pillars: Special A Yamada Nishiki, Kimoto and Yoshino Cedar

Taruzake is sake that has been left to rest for a time in a cedar barrel so that the aroma of the wood passes into it. For this limited edition, the barrels are made from cedar from Yoshino, known as one of Japan's leading cedar-producing regions, and the sake makes the most of its fragrance. The rice used is Yamada Nishiki alone, regarded as one of the rice varieties best suited to sake brewing. From among it, the brewery has chosen rice grown in the “Special A district”, the highest-ranked growing area.

The sake is brewed using a traditional method called “kimoto”. In this laborious technique, no lactic acid is added when preparing the shubo (the starter, a liquid in which the yeast that drives fermentation is multiplied); instead, the starter is raised slowly with the help of naturally occurring lactic acid bacteria. It is generally said to produce a taste with fullness and depth. The sake is said to be distinguished by a fresh fragrance, as if it had just been scooped from a newly opened barrel.

## Enjoying Barrel Sake in Europe

This run of 1,000 bottles can only be bought from the official online shop and the memorial museum, so it is probably difficult to obtain in Europe. However, the style itself, barrel sake carrying the scent of cedar, can still be enjoyed in Europe. At specialist sake shops, try looking for labels marked “Taruzake” or “cedar cask”. Chilling it too much closes up the cedar aroma, so we recommend drinking it at room temperature or lightly warmed. The woody fragrance also pairs well with smoked dishes, grilled meat, mushroom dishes and aged hard cheese. If you like wine or whisky matured in wooden casks, this may be a sake you find approachable. Bringing a bottle to a celebration could also give you something to talk about.
