## A mountain brand that evokes memories of travel

Hokkaido is a large island in the far north of Japan. Niseko, in its south-west, lies at the foot of Mount Yotei and is known for its many skiers from overseas. Yotei Brewing (Representative Director: Mariko Owa (大輪真理子)), an independent brewery based here, renamed its beer brand "Mountain Beer Supply Co." on 20 November 2026. The name carries the wish that people will think of the mountains even when they are drinking in town or at home, and the tagline is "Shaped by the Mountains". The branding was developed with CODO Design, based in Indianapolis in the US, which does a great deal of beverage packaging work. The logo takes as its motif the cloth flag known as a "pennant", once a staple souvenir of trips. The brewery says it hopes the brand will get drinkers talking about their own mountain memories, whether of skiing, climbing, camping or more.

## Three styles that capture the mountains in beer

"Expedition" is the brewery's most popular beer, a Hazy IPA it has brewed since its founding, and it replaces the old name "EXPDTN". It has a cloudy appearance, an aroma suggesting citrus and fully ripe tropical fruit, and a smooth mouthfeel. "Waymark" is a West Coast Pilsner that replaces the former "West Coast Lager"; citrus aromas rise from its creamy head, and it is light and easy to drink. "Bushwalk" is an IPA made with the New Zealand hop Nelson Sauvin, with a white-wine-like aroma reminiscent of Sauvignon Blanc and a fresh herbal note; the brewery itself recommends pairing it with fish dishes. The flagship pale ale "A Greener World" is not part of the new brand and will continue to be sold as before. It uses two hop varieties, Amarillo and Mosaic, and green energy is used in part of the brewing process.

## What European readers may want to note

Sales are currently centred on online stores and retailers within Japan, and besides the 350 mL cans, 13.5 L kegs will be available for restaurants and bars. No European distribution has been announced, but the brand is worth watching as an example of a small Japanese brewery adopting styles popular worldwide and building a brand around a local story. Beers made with Nelson Sauvin can sometimes be found in European craft beer shops, so pouring one into a wine glass and pairing it with white fish may give a clue to the flavour Bushwalk is aiming for. If you come across a Japanese craft beer, pay attention to where the brewery is located and which hops it uses.
