A sake brewed in Nagano Prefecture, a mountainous region in central Japan, has received high praise at a competition in the United States. "Imanishiki Junmai Ginjo Kinmonnishiki" (今錦 純米吟醸 金紋錦), brewed at the Imanishiki brewery run by Ina Food Industry of Nagano Prefecture and sold by its group company Yonezawa Shuzo, won the Grand Prix in the Ginjo category at the 2026 U.S. National Sake Appraisal. The trade newspaper Nihon Shokuryo Shimbun reported the news.

## The top score in its category among 486 entries

This year's appraisal was held in Honolulu, Hawaii, in the United States, from 20 to 22 October. A total of 486 sakes were entered by 153 breweries, and 140 Gold Awards and 129 Silver Awards were given. In addition, the sake with the highest score in each category receives the Grand Prix, and the runner-up receives the Semi-Grand Prix. Imanishiki's Junmai Ginjo earned this top honour in the Ginjo category. Including this bottle, five brands from the same producer won Gold Awards and four others won Silver Awards, a result that shows the high quality of the brewery as a whole, not just of a single bottle.

## What "Junmai Ginjo" and "Kinmonnishiki" mean

"Junmai Ginjo" on a sake label is one of the quality grades. "Junmai" means the sake is made only from rice, rice koji and water, with no brewer's alcohol added. "Ginjo" indicates that the outer part of the rice is polished away and only the core is used, with the remaining proportion of rice (the polishing ratio) set at 60% or less. The more of the outer layer is removed, the fewer off-flavours there are, and the more likely the sake is to have a floral aroma and a light taste. "Kinmonnishiki" in the brand name is the name of a variety of rice used for sake brewing.

## How to enjoy it in Europe

An award at an international competition is a useful guide when choosing good sake without travelling to Japan. When searching in specialist shops or online shops in Europe, look for the brewery name "Imanishiki" and the grade "Junmai Ginjo" on the label. Junmai Ginjo is best lightly chilled in the fridge and drunk from a wine glass, which lets the aroma open up. Paired with delicately flavoured dishes such as white fish carpaccio or soft cheese, it lets you enjoy the balance between the aroma and the umami of the rice. This award might also be a good reason to try Nagano sake and to compare different sake rice varieties side by side.
