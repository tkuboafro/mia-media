Asahi Shuzo, a sake brewery in Niigata Prefecture, served its flagship brands Kubota Manju and Kubota Manju Jisha Kobo Jikomi (brewed with the brewery's own yeast) at the Business of Luxury Summit – Asia Edition, an international conference co-hosted by Nikkei Inc. and the British business newspaper the Financial Times (FT). The conference was held in Bangkok, Thailand, on 19 and 20 October 2026. Following the first summit in Hong Kong in 2025, this is the second year in a row that the brewery has supplied its sake. At a gathering of the global luxury industry, sake took its place at the same table as the world's high-end brands.

## A conference of leaders from the world's luxury brands

The summit brings together executives from the luxury industry, brand heads, investors, entrepreneurs and cultural figures from around the world to discuss market trends and the creation of new value. This second edition took place at the Capella Bangkok hotel, with the Asia-Pacific luxury market, culture and hospitality as its themes. By letting participants taste the sake for themselves, Asahi Shuzo says it hopes to convey its brewing skill and its commitment to quality.

## The two Manju sakes and the brewery behind them

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://prcdn.freetls.fastly.net/release_image/28013/90/28013-90-61dbedd4dacb1162b9f9f104637c482e-3900x2600.jpg?width=1200&amp;height=900" alt="Kubota Manju, the top line of Asahi Shuzo" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Kubota Manju, the top line of Asahi Shuzo — Image: PR TIMES（朝日酒造） (press release)</figcaption></figure>

Kubota Manju, launched in 1986, is the top-tier sake of the Kubota brand. It has a showy aroma and a deep, layered flavour, and is known for an elegant character that does not overpower food. In 2026 it won a Gold Medal in the Junmai Daiginjo category of Kura Master 2026, a sake competition held in France. Junmai daiginjo is one of the most labour-intensive grades of sake: it is made only from rice polished down from the outside to 50% or less of its original weight, together with rice koji and water.

The other sake, Kubota Manju Jisha Kobo Jikomi, is a junmai daiginjo made with particular attention to the variety of sake rice, the degree of polishing and the brewery's own yeast. It is said to offer layer upon layer of graceful aroma, a deep and mellow taste, and a clean finish that fades away crisply.

Asahi Shuzo was founded in 1830 in Nagaoka, in Niigata Prefecture, a famed rice-growing region on the Sea of Japan coast. Following the principle that "sake brewing begins with rice growing", it cultivates sake rice together with local farmers, and draws on soft groundwater and the traditional skills of its master brewers (toji).

## How to enjoy it in Europe

If you are looking in Europe, look for the name "Kubota Manju" on the lists of specialist sake shops and restaurants. Kura Master is a competition judged by sommeliers and others in France, so its awards can also be a useful guide for people used to wine. Because aroma matters so much in junmai daiginjo, it is easier to appreciate its bouquet if you drink it from a wine glass rather than a small ochoko cup, and not too cold. Pairing it with white fish, shellfish or dishes with delicate sauces is one idea.
