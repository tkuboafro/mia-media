People with dysphagia, a condition that makes it hard to swallow food and drink, can easily choke on thin, watery liquids, and joining in when alcohol is served can be difficult. To make a sake that such people can enjoy alongside everyone else, a brewery in Gifu and a doctor have joined forces. Gifu Prefecture lies in the Chubu region, north of Nagoya, and is known for its clear streams and rice-growing country.

## A Brewery and a Swallowing Specialist Join Forces

The project is being developed by Nihon Izumi Shuzo, a brewery in Kano Shimizu-cho, Gifu City, and Kenjiro Kunieda (47), a doctor who specialises in swallowing disorders. Their motto is "Even with a disability, let's raise a toast together." Their goal is to make it possible for people whose ability to swallow has weakened through illness or age to enjoy a drink at the same table as family and friends. It is an attempt to combine knowledge from medical practice with brewing skill.

## Thickness, the Scent of Yuzu and a Crunchy Texture

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/49868309128_e29b09e97f_b.jpg" alt="Close-up of yuzu fruit (an ingredient evoking the aroma of the thickened, frozen sake)" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Close-up of yuzu fruit (an ingredient evoking the aroma of the thickened, frozen sake) — Photo: Goat Tree Designs (flickr, CC BY)</figcaption></figure>

The product is called "Frozen Thickened SAKE". Thickener is added to the sake, which is then given the aroma of yuzu, a strongly scented Japanese citrus fruit, and frozen so that it can be enjoyed as a crunchy, sorbet-like treat. In care settings, thickening drinks to slow their passage down the throat is a widely used technique, and the idea has been applied here to alcohol. The prototype has an alcohol content of 12 to 14%, slightly lower than typical sake (around 15 to 16%). As of the August 2026 report, it was still at the development stage ahead of commercialisation.

## How to Enjoy It in Europe, and What to Watch

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/6952740/pexels-photo-6952740.jpeg?auto=compress&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;dpr=2&amp;h=650&amp;w=940" alt="People around a table clinking glasses in a toast" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>People around a table clinking glasses in a toast — Photo: cottonbro studio (Pexels)</figcaption></figure>

This sake is not currently available in Europe, but the notion that anyone can drink together is what deserves attention. In Europe too, where populations are ageing, "inclusive drinks" tailored to physical condition could become the next theme after no- and low-alcohol drinks. Freezing sake into a sorbet is something you can try at home, and adding yuzu peel or its aroma turns it into a summery, dessert-like glass. Alongside the progress of commercialisation, we will keep watching how this small Gifu brewery's question develops.
