Takagi Shoten (formerly named Isonosawa), the former corporate entity of a long-established maker of sake and shochu in Ukiha, Fukuoka Prefecture, in northern Kyushu, received a decision to commence special liquidation from the Kurume branch of the Fukuoka District Court on 24 August 2026. Special liquidation is a procedure in which a joint-stock company that has already been dissolved settles its assets and debts under the supervision of a court. Although the word "bankruptcy" appeared in headlines, the brewery itself has not disappeared. The brewing business was transferred to a separate company in 2021, and the present Isonosawa carries on trading, unaffected by this liquidation.

## From 2.9 billion yen to 1.1 billion yen: a shrinking domestic market

According to Tokyo Shoko Research's Fukuoka branch, the company was founded in 1893 and incorporated in 1948. It produced the sake brands Isonosawa and Shun, as well as shochu, and had built sales channels with major and influential local food wholesalers. Sales peaked at about 2.9 billion yen in the fiscal year ending June 2001. After that, revenue kept falling because of population decline and changes in lifestyle, dropping to about 1.1 billion yen in the fiscal year ending June 2019. Weak demand for sake, compounded by the COVID-19 pandemic, is said to have worsened its finances further.

## Separating the business to keep the brewery

The company chose to rebuild through a transfer of the business to a third party. A new company was established in December 2020 under the name Ukiha Shoten, and on 1 June 2021 it changed its name to Isonosawa and took over the brewing business. In its business purposes, the new company added the running of restaurants and accommodation to the manufacture and sale of alcoholic beverages and food. The former entity changed its name to Takagi Shoten on the same day and effectively went dormant. A shareholders' meeting on 30 April 2026 resolved to dissolve it, leading to the present special liquidation. At the time of reporting, the total amount of debt was still under investigation.

## Reading behind the "bankruptcy" headline

Among Japan's regional sake breweries, restructurings that separate an indebted old company from a new company taking over the brand and equipment are carried out fairly often. This case follows that pattern, and the headline alone could lead readers to think that the brewery has gone. In fact, the brand and the place where it is brewed continue under new management.

When choosing sake in Europe, knowing the background to such restructurings helps you avoid misunderstandings. The producer's name and address on a bottle's back label tell you which company is making the sake today. Kyushu is known as a shochu-producing region, but there are breweries such as the maker of Isonosawa that have also produced sake. If you come across this brewery's name, spare a thought for the path a regional sake brewery chose in order to survive.
