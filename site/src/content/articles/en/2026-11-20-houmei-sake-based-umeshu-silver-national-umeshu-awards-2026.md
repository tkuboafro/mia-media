## Silver at the National Umeshu Competition

<figure class="embed embed--social"><blockquote class="twitter-tweet"><a href="https://x.com/houmeisyuzo/status/2123700210954391797">https://x.com/houmeisyuzo/status/2123700210954391797</a></blockquote></figure>

Houmei Shuzo, a sake brewery in Tamba-Sasayama, Hyogo Prefecture, announced its award on its official X account on 20 November 2026. Its "Houmei Umeshu Nihonshu Jikomi" (sake-based umeshu) was awarded silver in the "Sake Umeshu category" of the 11th National Umeshu Competition 2026. The National Umeshu Competition is a contest that judges umeshu from all over Japan, and it includes a category specifically for umeshu made with a sake base. In its post, the brewery said it was delighted that "we managed to place this year", which suggests this was a result it had long been waiting for.

## Umeshu Brewed with Sake

Umeshu is a traditional Japanese liqueur made by steeping ume fruit (a Japanese fruit closely related to the apricot) in alcohol together with sugar. It is such a familiar drink in Japan that many people make it at home. The base is usually shochu (a Japanese distilled spirit) or a neutral, colourless distilled spirit. By contrast, umeshu made with sake, a brewed alcohol made by fermenting rice, as its base is known as "sake umeshu". What makes this style distinctive is the way the rice-derived savoury depth (umami) and soft mouthfeel of sake combine with the tartness and aroma of the ume.

## Founded in 1797, a Brewery in the Home of the Tamba Toji

Houmei Shuzo was founded in 1797 (Kansei 9 in the Japanese era calendar) and has a history of more than 200 years. Tamba-Sasayama is a town in the hills of inland Hyogo Prefecture, north of Osaka and Kobe. The area is known as the home of the "Tamba Toji". A toji is the master brewer who leads the making of sake, and the Tamba Toji are one of Japan's leading schools of toji. A brewery in a region where the tradition of sake-making runs so deep has now also earned recognition for an umeshu that draws on sake.

In Europe, sake-based umeshu is still not often seen. When looking for umeshu at Japanese food shops or specialist sake importers, check whether the label or product description says "sake-brewed" or "sake base". Competition awards can also be a useful guide when choosing. The classic way to enjoy it is as an after-dinner drink, well chilled, either neat or on the rocks. Topped up with sparkling water, it also makes a light aperitif. Comparing it with a shochu-based umeshu may let you notice the difference in the rice-derived umami.
