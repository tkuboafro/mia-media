Umenoyado Shuzo, a sake brewery in Nara Prefecture, listed its shares on the Standard Market of the Tokyo Stock Exchange on 24 April 2026. The Standard Market is the segment for mid-sized companies among the exchange's three market categories. Most sake breweries in Japan are family-run, and few of them raise money on the stock market. Nara is a prefecture in western Japan, next to Osaka and Kyoto, and has a long tradition of sake brewing. At a press conference on the day of the listing, president Kayo Yoshida set out a policy of putting more effort into overseas sales of liqueurs made with sake as their base.

## A first day well above the offer price

The offer price, fixed before the listing, was 600 yen per share. The first price struck once trading began (the opening price) was 900 yen, 50% higher. The closing price rose further, to 1,050 yen. The first day's movement suggests that investors have high expectations for the brewery's growth. Yoshida expressed her gratitude for the high regard shown by investors and said the company would redouble its efforts from here.

## Sake liqueurs and new fields

Umenoyado Shuzo's core business is making and selling liqueurs that use sake. Japanese fruit liqueurs are often based on shochu or distilled alcohol, but with sake as the base, the soft umami that comes from rice tends to blend with the aroma of the fruit. Yoshida said the company will strengthen sales of these liqueurs overseas and also move into new fields such as food. The stock market listing is a step towards pursuing this growth strategy.

## How to enjoy it in Europe

Sake liqueur is an approachable drink even for people who do not yet know sake well. It can be drunk well chilled on its own, on the rocks with ice, or mixed with soda. It suits an aperitif just as well as an after-dinner glass to accompany dessert. When buying, check the label to see whether it is sake-based; this lets you compare it side by side with shochu-based liqueurs. It will be interesting to see whether the listing means we see the Umenoyado name more often in European drinks shops and bars.
