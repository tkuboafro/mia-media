## Why a long-established brewery is refining the "everyday glass"

<figure class="embed embed--video"><iframe src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/cWYqcxnK5Fk" title="YouTube video" loading="lazy" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" allowfullscreen referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin"></iframe></figure>

Yoshinokawa is a long-established sake brewery in Nagaoka, in Niigata Prefecture, a rice-growing region on the Sea of Japan coast. In Niigata it is widely enjoyed as an everyday sake to drink with dinner, and it is also a familiar sight in restaurants and shops in Tokyo and other cities. The autumn 2026 rebrand revisited the logo and corporate image, the flavour and the bottle design, and covered almost the entire range. It was led by Masashi Kawakami, 30, a managing director from the founding family. After working for a major beverage company, he joined the brewery in 2023 and took forward a plan that had long been in the pipeline. Kawakami says: "Special bottles matter, but the brewery's mission is to keep making everyday sake and to increase the number of people who drink it." He adds that there is a sense of crisis too: if everyday sake disappears, sake culture itself will decline.

## Four-season brewing and chilled storage behind "Umai Tanrei"

<figure class="embed embed--video"><iframe src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/J5vr8hAf9cI" title="YouTube video" loading="lazy" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" allowfullscreen referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin"></iframe></figure>

The new guiding principle for flavour is "Umai Tanrei" (delicious and light). The brewery has kept the light, clean character of "tanrei" that is typical of Niigata sake, and defined the style as a sake in which a refined sweetness, gentle acidity and umami are balanced, with a clean finish. To produce this flavour consistently, the brewery strengthened its air-conditioning and cooling equipment. It abandoned the former method of brewing in three seasons, excluding summer, and switched to "shiki jozo" (four-season brewing), brewing all year round, so that maturation can be managed throughout the year. It has also introduced "tare-tsubo", vessels that receive freshly pressed sake, which limit oxidation and protect the flavour. In addition, it has built a new refrigerated warehouse that can store bottles at 5°C, raising its bottle storage capacity from 50,000 to up to 300,000 bottles, counted as 720 ml bottles. All "tokutei meisho-shu" (premium grades of sake classified by ingredients and the degree to which the rice is polished) are stored in bottle.

## "Seiryubi" for food and the lighter "Keiryubi"

<figure class="embed embed--video"><iframe src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/GwSzfFeFt-I" title="YouTube video" loading="lazy" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" allowfullscreen referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin"></iframe></figure>

At the heart of the new line-up are two series positioned as "daily sake". "Seiryubi" is a sake to drink with meals: its aroma is gentle, yet it has body and umami, and a clean finish. It comprises three products: the main Junmai Ginjo, joined by a Junmai Daiginjo and a Junmai. "Keiryubi" is low in alcohol and characterised by a fruit-like aroma. It has two products: an easy-drinking Junmai and a Junmai Sparkling with fine bubbles. The brewery's former flagship, "Gensen Karakuchi", also remains, with a new design. Kawakami says he wants to go to popular restaurants and to events unrelated to sake, so that younger people unfamiliar with sake can also try it.

What will interest European readers is that this renewal is aimed not at premium sake but at sake for ordinary drinking. Niigata's light, clean sake pairs easily with everyday meals such as grilled fish, grilled vegetables and salty dishes, and when served chilled in a wine glass its delicate sweetness and acidity come through. Low-alcohol sake and sparkling sake also suit an aperitif. Alongside label designations such as "Junmai Ginjo" and "Junmai", it is worth checking whether the sake has been kept chilled in the bottle, which reveals the modern brewery's emphasis on freshness.
