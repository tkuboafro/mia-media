## A yamahai ginjo junmai takes the top prize, with Okayama's Omachi as runner-up

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://prcdn.freetls.fastly.net/release_image/100239/21/100239-21-d20ab52e757d83d346ebff478d6b1c02-1120x1120.jpg?width=1200&amp;height=900" alt="Official image accompanying the announcement of the MONACO SAKE AWARD 2026 winners" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Official image accompanying the announcement of the MONACO SAKE AWARD 2026 winners — Image: PR TIMES (press release)</figcaption></figure>

The results of MONACO SAKE AWARD 2026, a sake competition held in the small Mediterranean Principality of Monaco, have been announced. The first round of judging took place at the Le Méridien hotel with the help of sommeliers from the Monaco Sommelier Association. The final judging was held on 8 October at the Yacht Club de Monaco. The judges were all Monaco residents, including members of the princely family, court chefs, and sommeliers from top hotels and restaurants. In total, 33 brands won awards, and counting sake that collected more than one prize, there were 53 awards in all.

The Grand Prix went to ‘Yamahai Ginjo Junmai’, brewed by Kasumitsuru of Hyogo Prefecture. Yamahai is a traditional method in which the shubo (the fermentation starter) is patiently cultivated using lactic acid bacteria taken in from the air and surroundings, and it tends to produce sake with body and acidity. As well as the Grand Prix, this sake also won the Gold Award in the Junmai Ginjo/Ginjo category, the Monaco Sommelier Association Award and the Mariage Award. The runner-up for the Grand Prix was ‘Akaiwa Omachi Junmai Daiginjo’ from Toshimori Shuzo in Okayama Prefecture. Omachi is a sake rice variety that has long been grown in Okayama.

## Thirteen sakes to pair with porcini: the Mariage Award

The theme of the Mariage Award, which rewards compatibility with food, was porcini mushrooms, a signature ingredient of the European autumn. Thirteen sakes were selected. From Niizawa Brewery in Miyagi Prefecture, two were chosen: ‘Hakurakusei Junmai Daiginjo Hikari’ and ‘Moehime Junmai Daiginjo’. Yamaguchi Prefecture contributed ‘Dassai Junmai Daiginjo Migaki Niwari Sanbu’ and ‘Gokyo Junmai Daiginjo 50%’. Junmai daiginjo is a category in which the rice is polished down to half its original size or less (a polishing ratio of 50% or lower) and no distilled alcohol is added. ‘Migaki Niwari Sanbu’ means the rice has been polished down to 23% of its original size. ‘Yamahai Junmai Daiginjo 2014BY’ from Tsubijin Shuzo in Hyogo Prefecture is an aged sake brewed in the 2014 brewing year, which means it has been matured for more than ten years.

## From aged sake to liqueur: a wide range of winners

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://prcdn.freetls.fastly.net/release_image/100239/21/100239-21-aff0545e81b669356208d9cf70f76527-1120x1120.jpg?width=1200&amp;height=900" alt="Official announcement image for MONACO SAKE AWARD 2026" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Official announcement image for MONACO SAKE AWARD 2026 — Image: PR TIMES (press release)</figcaption></figure>

Niizawa Brewery took three of the 13 Gold Awards in the Junmai Daiginjo/Daiginjo category. Besides its Grand Prix sake, Kasumitsuru also won Gold in the Futsushu (regular sake) category with ‘Kimoto Karakuchi’ and in the Sake & Variety category with ‘Yamahai 25-degree’, which is 25% ABV. In the Fruit Wine category, Gold Awards went to a plum wine made with sake by Gassan Shuzo of Yamagata Prefecture and to ‘Born Yuzu Liqueur’ by Kato Kichibei Shoten of Fukui Prefecture. ‘Junmai Daiginjo Chokaisan’ by Tenju Shuzo of Akita Prefecture also won a Silver Award.

If you are looking for these award-winning sakes in Europe, the words ‘Yamahai’ and ‘Kimoto’ on the label are a useful guide. Both are traditional methods that give sake firm acidity and umami, so you can expect them to pair well with porcini risotto or mushroom pasta. They can also be enjoyed at room temperature or gently warmed to around 40°C, with the aroma checked in a wine glass. The elegant junmai daiginjo styles, meanwhile, are best served chilled as an aperitif, so there is also the pleasure of choosing a different temperature for each sake.
