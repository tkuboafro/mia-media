The Japanese craft gin HAKUBA GIN has won the top prize, "Red Dot: Best of the Best", at the "Red Dot Award: Brands & Communication Design 2026", organised by Red Dot, which is based in Essen, Germany. The winners are "CIRCULAR DESIGN STUDIO.", an in-house team at the Tokyo advertising agency Shinto Tsushin Co., Ltd., and the design company Design Person Co., Ltd. The award ceremony was held on 6 November 2026 at the Konzerthaus in Berlin.

## Bottling today's forest amid climate change

HAKUBA GIN is set in Hakuba Village in Nagano Prefecture, a village surrounded by mountains in the northern Japanese Alps. Its concept is to record, as scent, the present state of Hakuba's forest, which is changing because of climate change, and to deliver it to people. CIRCULAR DESIGN STUDIO. is a team that specialises in the circular economy (an economy in which resources are kept in circulation), and the gin grew out of "GREEN WORK HAKUBA", a business conference it co-hosts with the Hakuba Village Tourism Bureau. It was developed jointly with Ethical Spirits Co., Ltd., a Japanese spirits maker, and went on sale on 18 February 2026.

The botanicals (the plants used for flavouring) are eight species that grow wild in the village. They include shishiudo, a member of the carrot family (Apiaceae), as well as nanakamado (rowan), kumazasa (a dwarf bamboo) and koshiabura, which is also known as an edible wild vegetable. Part of the proceeds goes towards nature conservation efforts in Hakuba Village.

## A design that lets you see the forest through the bottle

What the jury praised was the idea behind the packaging. The label states the date, place and temperature at which the plants were gathered. On the back of the bottle is a label printed on both sides with a photograph of the forest where the plants were picked, so that the clear gin makes it look as if you are gazing at the forest scenery through the liquid. Red Dot is an international design award founded in 1955, and the 2026 Brands & Communication Design category was judged by 30 experts from fields such as design and academia. In the same year the gin also won awards in three categories at the New York ADC Awards in New York, including a Silver Cube, and the prizes keep coming.

## How to enjoy it in Europe, and what to watch for

For gin lovers in Europe, the interesting point is the link with familiar plants. Shishiudo is a relative of angelica, a classic gin ingredient, and there are also species close to nanakamado in Europe. If you get the chance to try it, comparing how plants from a Japanese mountain smell and taste in the glass with the harvest date and temperature on the label is a pleasure unique to this gin. The second edition, made with plants growing wild in the grounds of the Hakuba Goryu Alpine Botanical Garden with the garden's cooperation, is scheduled to go on sale around December 2026. How the forest's "record" changes from year to year is another thing to look forward to.
