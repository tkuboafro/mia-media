When people think of Japanese spirits, many picture whisky. Yet small craft gin distilleries are now springing up all over Japan. The gin from Tokachi Heiya Distillery (Tokachi Heiya Distillery), in the Tokachi region of eastern Hokkaido, has received the top gold award at the spirits competition Tokyo Whisky & Spirits Competition (TWSC) 2026. The local newspaper Tokachi Mainichi Shimbun reports the news.

## A craft gin born on the Tokachi plain

The Tokachi region lies in eastern Hokkaido and is known as one of Japan's leading agricultural areas. Its broad plain produces potatoes, beans, wheat, dairy products and more, and it is sometimes called Japan's "pantry". Tokachi Heiya Distillery is a company in the town of Makubetsu (Makubetsu) in this region, making and selling craft gin; its president is Yoshihiro Miyazawa (Yoshihiro Miyazawa). In the photo accompanying the report, President Miyazawa holds the award certificate together with three bottles of gin: "明時 Tanzanite", "Cassis" and "Origin". The gins were reportedly praised at the competition for making good use of local ingredients.

## What kind of competition is the TWSC?

The TWSC is a competition held in Tokyo for spirits from Japan and abroad, including whisky, gin and shochu. Judges taste the entries blind, with the brand names concealed, and score them category by category. The top gold award goes to the spirits that earn particularly high marks among them. For gin lovers in Europe, an award like this offers a useful clue for discovering a small, new Japanese distillery. Many Japanese craft gins use Japanese botanicals such as citrus, tea and sansho pepper, and can offer aromas quite different from those of a London Dry Gin.

## Enjoying it in Europe

With Dutch genever, Spain's gin tonic culture and the craft gin boom in Germany and Britain, Europe is an especially big market for gin. If you come across a gin from Hokkaido, try smelling it first neat, at room temperature or slightly chilled, and then mix it with a tonic of gentle aroma; the character of the ingredients may then come through more clearly. A bottle with a fruit in its name, such as Cassis, may also work well with soda or as an aperitif. When browsing the lists of specialist shops and importers that handle Japanese gin, look out for the names "Hokkaido" and "Tokachi", and for a TWSC award.
