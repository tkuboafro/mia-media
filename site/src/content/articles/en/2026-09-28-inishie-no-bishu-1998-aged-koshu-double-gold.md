Most sake is drunk fresh, within about a year of pressing. Sake that is instead left to mature for decades is called "koshu" (aged sake), and it is winning growing international acclaim. Takumi Sosei Co., Ltd., part of the major staffing and services company Pasona Group, has its head office on Awaji Island in Hyogo Prefecture, an island to the south-west of Kobe. On 2 July 2026, the company launched a new release, "Inishie no Bishu 1998", in Japan under its aged-koshu brand "Inishie no Bishu".

## 28 years of maturation, gold at two international competitions

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://prcdn.freetls.fastly.net/release_image/16751/2253/16751-2253-1685a050066a2e6990d8525163e6d2f4-207x446.jpg?width=1200&amp;height=900" alt="The 375 ml bottle of the 28-year-aged koshu &quot;Inishie no Bishu 1998&quot;" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>The 375 ml bottle of the 28-year-aged koshu &quot;Inishie no Bishu 1998&quot; — Image: PR TIMES（パソナグループ） (press release)</figcaption></figure>

This sake has been matured for about 28 years, and won gold at two international competitions in 2026. One is the International Wine Challenge (IWC), a blind-tasting competition founded in 1984. IWC added a sake category in 2007 and it has become the largest sake competition held outside Japan. The gold medal was awarded in the aged-sake (koshu) class of that sake category. The other is the aged-sake category of Kura Master 2026, a France-based sake competition judged mainly by French professionals. It is notable that European judges rated an aged sake so highly. In November 2023, Takumi Sosei opened "Nihon no Koshu-gura", a shared storage facility dedicated to aged sake. There it handles everything from storage to bottling, shipping and product development, and it also works to support breweries and build sales channels.

## Aromas of honey, apricot and caramel

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/7254446/pexels-photo-7254446.jpeg?auto=compress&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;dpr=2&amp;h=650&amp;w=940" alt="A tasting glass filled with amber-coloured aged sake (illustrative image)" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>A tasting glass filled with amber-coloured aged sake (illustrative image) — Photo: Eva Bronzini (Pexels)</figcaption></figure>

According to the announcement, the moment the sake is poured into the glass, a honeyed sweetness rises along with a calm aroma reminiscent of dry oak casks. The bouquet is complex, with layers of yellow peach and apricot compote, caramel and a hint of spice. The texture is smooth, and as it develops the savoury umami of the rice spreads quietly, followed by a long finish with a pleasant bitterness. The aromas created by maturation are also the subject of scientific research. Compounds such as sotolon, which recalls caramel and dried fruit, and diethyl succinate, with its honey-like scent, are known.

## How to enjoy it in Europe

"Inishie no Bishu 1998" comes in a 375 ml bottle, with an alcohol content of 18%, and sells in Japan for 13,200 yen. No plans for sales in Europe have been announced at present. Many koshu print the vintage on the label, as with wine. When searching in Europe, look for the words "koshu" or "jukuseishu" (aged sake) together with a year. The lists of winners in the aged-sake categories at IWC and Kura Master can also be a useful guide. Do not over-chill it, and sip it a little at a time from a small wine glass. Enjoyed as an after-dinner drink, like sherry or old port, it makes it easier to appreciate the interplay of sweetness and bitterness.
