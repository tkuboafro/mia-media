## Three Brews in the Top Four of the First International Craft Sake Award

<figure class="embed embed--social"><blockquote class="twitter-tweet"><a href="https://x.com/tachikawa_tetsu/status/2121230395270451311">https://x.com/tachikawa_tetsu/status/2121230395270451311</a></blockquote></figure>

On 13 November 2026, Tetsuyuki Tachikawa, representative of Pukupuku Brewing, which makes doburoku and craft sake in Hamadori, the Pacific coastal region of Fukushima Prefecture, shared the news on his own X account. In the DOBUROKU category of the WORLD CRAFT SAKE AWARD 2026, a craft sake competition held for the first time this year, "Pukupuku Hop VaVaVa" passed the preliminary round in first place. What is more, Pukupuku Brewing took three of the top four places in the category. Judging is blind: brand names are hidden and the entries are assessed on taste alone. It was the first time Tachikawa had entered a competition of this kind. The final, which includes a presentation, is still to come.

## What Are Craft Sake and Doburoku?

Like sake, craft sake is made from rice and koji (rice on which koji mould has been cultivated, the key ingredient in fermentation), but it is brewed outside the confines of conventional sake, with additions such as fruit, herbs and hops. Over the past few years it has been growing in Japan as a new category. Because it is difficult to obtain a new licence to produce seishu (sake) in Japan, many of the new brewers work under a different type of liquor licence, which has encouraged freedom of invention. Doburoku, on the other hand, is a rustic drink in which the moromi (fermenting mash) is not pressed, leaving the texture of the rice grains and the cloudiness in the finished product. Pukupuku Brewing makes all of its drinks without adding yeast, with a crossover between sake and craft beer as its theme. VaVaVa, whose name carries the word "hop", is a bottle that captures this direction well.

## Six Years of Work and a Coincidental Release Date

About six years ago, Tachikawa took part in setting up haccoba, a craft sake brewery in Fukushima Prefecture, and began making craft sake as its first head brewer. He looks back on the years since, saying he has kept brewing with the conviction that he will not be beaten on technique or taste. He added that this result is thanks to his colleagues at the brewery, the rice farmers and everyone who supports him. By coincidence, "Pukupuku Hop VaVaVa AUTUMN 2026", from the same lot as the award-winning brew, goes on sale the very next day, 14 November. Tachikawa himself laughs that it is purely a coincidence.

In Europe, shops that stock craft sake and doburoku are still limited. For now, it is not known whether this brew will be available there. What to watch first is the result of the final, and whether this inaugural award will establish itself as an international benchmark for craft sake. At sake specialists and online shops, try searching with terms such as "Craft Sake", "Doburoku" and "Nigori". Some doburoku continues to ferment in the bottle, so keep it refrigerated and take care when opening it, as it may foam over. For a hop-scented style, one idea is to drink it well chilled from a tulip-shaped beer glass. For anyone familiar with Belgian or German beer culture, it may prove a new way into Japanese drinks.
