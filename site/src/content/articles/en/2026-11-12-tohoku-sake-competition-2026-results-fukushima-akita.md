The Sendai Regional Taxation Bureau, a regional office of Japan's National Tax Agency, announced the results of the Tohoku Sake Appraisal on 10 November. Tohoku comprises six prefectures in northern Honshu and is one of Japan's leading sake-producing regions. The appraisal is an official judging event intended to raise the brewing skills and quality of breweries in the six prefectures. In the ginjo category, Kitashika of Odate, Akita Prefecture (flagship brand: Kitashika), was named the top brewery, while in the junmai category the top brewery was Higashi Nihon Shuzo Kyogyo Kumiai of Nihonmatsu, Fukushima Prefecture (flagship brand: Okunomatsu).

## Judging sake matured over the summer

The sake judged is mainly that brewed in the previous winter and matured in storage for about six months. The judges assessed aroma and flavour from the end of September to early October, and they included foreign experts. The addition of overseas judges is thought to have brought a perspective closer to that of drinkers in export markets. Ginjo is a fragrant sake made from rice that has been polished to remove its outer layers and fermented slowly at low temperatures. Junmai refers to sake made only from rice, rice koji and water, with no added brewer's alcohol.

According to the judges' comments, Kitashika's ginjo was distinguished by the mature character typical of an autumn sake that has come through the summer, and was praised for its calm, sweet aroma reminiscent of honey and peach and its rich, full-bodied flavour. Okunomatsu's junmai has a soft aroma recalling pineapple, pear, apple and white peach, with a good balance of sweetness and acidity, and a slight astringency that tightens its smooth texture, the judges said.

## Fukushima and Akita lead by prefecture

The Judges' Special Prize, which ranks next after the top award, went in the ginjo category to Nakayu Shuzoten (Tenjo Mugen) of Kami, Miyagi Prefecture, and Suzuki Shuzoten (Hideyoshi) of Daisen, Akita Prefecture. In the junmai category, it went to Akabu Shuzo (AKABU) of Morioka, Iwate Prefecture, and Ichinokura Honsha Kura (Ichinokura Shoko) of Osaki, Miyagi Prefecture.

The Excellence Award was given to 37 of the 103 breweries (115 entries) in the ginjo category and 44 of the 125 breweries (152 entries) in the junmai category. Counting both categories together, Fukushima had the most award-winning breweries by prefecture with 15. Akita followed with 14, Yamagata with 12 and Iwate with 8, while Aomori and Miyagi had 7 each.

## How to enjoy them in Europe

Some of this year's award-winning breweries, such as Okunomatsu, Ichinokura and AKABU, already have brands that can be found in specialist sake shops and online shops in Europe. When choosing a bottle, check whether the label says "junmai" or "ginjo", and try tasting with the fruit aromas mentioned in the judges' comments in mind. A mature autumn ginjo is best poured into a white wine glass and served not too cold, at a slightly higher temperature, which helps the aroma open up. It also pairs easily with matured cheese and white meat dishes.
