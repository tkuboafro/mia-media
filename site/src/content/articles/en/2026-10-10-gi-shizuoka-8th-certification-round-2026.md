Shizuoka Prefecture sits on the Pacific coast roughly halfway between Tokyo and Nagoya, and is also known as the region spreading out at the foot of Mount Fuji. On 20 April 2026, the Shizuoka Sake Brewers Association, made up of breweries in the prefecture, held a review session for "GI Shizuoka" certification and announced the newly certified seiko (sake). This is the eighth round of certification. It is a less eye-catching topic than glamorous award news, but it offers a clue to how place-of-origin labelling for sake actually works.

## What is GI Shizuoka?

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/37849772/pexels-photo-37849772.jpeg?auto=compress&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;dpr=2&amp;h=650&amp;w=940" alt="An image of a sake label bearing a Geographical Indication (GI)" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>An image of a sake label bearing a Geographical Indication (GI) — Photo: Iban Lopez Luna (Pexels)</figcaption></figure>

A Geographical Indication (GI) is a system for protecting the name of a place for products whose quality and character are closely tied to where they come from. The idea is close to the appellations of origin used for European wine and cheese, and in Japan the national government designates GIs for alcoholic drinks. "Shizuoka" is one of them. Under GI Shizuoka, a sake cannot use the name simply because it was brewed within the prefecture; it must be certified at a review session. In other words, "Shizuoka" on a label indicates that the sake has been checked against set standards.

## What was decided at the 20 April review

According to the association's announcement, new sake was certified as GI Shizuoka at the review session on 20 April. This announcement marks the eighth round of certification, and the latest batch joins the sake certified so far. The list of certified brands is published on the association's official website, where you can check which sake from which brewery was included.

## What repeated rounds tell us

The number eight shows that GI is not a signboard hung up once and forgotten, but a system kept running through continued review. The association also supports technical development for ginjo sake (a fragrant sake made from rice polished heavily and fermented slowly at low temperature) and works on improving and developing the prefecture's own sake-brewing rice. GI certification can be said to play a role in communicating the results of this region-wide effort to quality to people outside the prefecture.

When choosing Shizuoka sake in Europe, look at the back label of the bottle or the importer's product description for "GI Shizuoka" or "地理的表示 静岡". Like the AOP or DOP on a wine, it is a guide that the sake meets the standards of its place of origin. Once you have a bottle in hand, you might try nosing it in a white wine glass. Comparing it with sake from other GI regions may reveal what individual character each sake that carries the name of its place of origin brings to the table.
