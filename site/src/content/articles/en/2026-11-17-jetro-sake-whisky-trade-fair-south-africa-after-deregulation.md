## Around 80 buyers attend in Johannesburg

On 26 October 2026, JETRO (the Japan External Trade Organization, a Japanese government-affiliated trade promotion agency) held a business meeting in Johannesburg, South Africa's largest commercial city, together with the Embassy of Japan in the Republic of South Africa. The venue was the restaurant The Prawnery, which already stocks Japanese drinks, as does the neighbouring bar KANPAI. In recent years South Africa has simplified the import procedures for sake and relaxed its rules on the container sizes in which it may be sold. According to related JETRO reporting, this means sake can now be sold at retail in any container size. In response to these changes, the event aimed to create opportunities for deals and raise awareness of sake. Whisky, which is very popular with local buyers, was also included.

Eleven Japanese companies took part. These included firms that did not travel from Japan but were represented by agents already based in South Africa. Another 11 companies that could not attend had their products displayed only. On the South African side, around 80 people from about 50 companies came, mainly from retail and restaurants. Products from absent companies were presented on their behalf by students on a local sake sommelier training course, and online business meetings were also held in the afternoon.

## Stories and food overcome the price barrier

On the day, a masterclass was also given by a sommelier involved in sake education in South Africa. After an introduction to production methods, regions and history, participants compared the differences in taste between brands while pairing them with the restaurant's fusion dishes. One buyer said: "It is more expensive than local drinks, but once you learn the story of the region and the background, you can understand its value, and I would like to stock it." A buyer who was trying sake for the first time said that on its own it tasted strongly alcoholic with a deep flavour, but that it became more approachable when paired with food. On the 27th, the participating companies visited the food importer and wholesaler Rialto Foods to hear about its storage and distribution set-up for Japanese and Asian foods, as well as market trends.

## What to note from a European perspective

The reaction in South Africa suggests what works in a market where sake is still new. A high price can turn into acceptance through the story of the region and the brewer, and through pairing with food. The same is likely to be true in Europe. When choosing a bottle, it is worth checking the region and the brewery's background on the back label or in the retailer's description. If you are new to sake, we recommend pairing it with seafood or strongly aromatic dishes rather than drinking it on its own. It is also worth remembering that Japanese producers are expanding their sales channels into Africa. As export destinations diversify, this may affect the range of brands and styles that reach Europe.
