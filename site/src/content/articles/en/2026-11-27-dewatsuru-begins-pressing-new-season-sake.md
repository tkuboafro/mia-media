## The Sugidama Announces: "This Year's Sake Is Ready"

Akita Prefecture is a rice-growing region on the Sea of Japan side of northern Honshu. Dewatsuru Shuzo is a sake brewery in Nangai, in the inland city of Daisen. According to the local newspaper Akita Sakigake Shimpo, the brewery began pressing this season's new sake on 16 November, and a fresh green sugidama was hung from its eaves. A sugidama is a ball made of bundled cedar leaves, and it marks a sake brewery. The new green sugidama is a signal that new sake has been made. As time passes the leaves wither and turn brown, so the change in colour has long been compared to the way sake matures. The new sake was scheduled to be shipped from around the 25th to destinations in and outside Akita Prefecture.

## What Is "Pressing"? The Colour and Aroma of New Sake

Sake is made by pressing moromi, the mash produced by fermenting rice, rice koji and water. Pressing separates it into liquid sake and solid sake kasu (sake lees). This step is called "shibori" (pressing), and sake taken straight after pressing is called "shiboritate" (freshly pressed). In its headline, the Akita Sakigake Shimpo described this season's new sake as "aosae", with a pale green tinge, and reported that the brewery was filled with a refreshing aroma. Young sake that has not yet been filtered or heat-pasteurised (hiire) can retain a very faint colour. Its aroma also tends to be lively, and in Japan too it is looked forward to as a character that can be enjoyed only at the start of the season.

## How to Enjoy New Sake in Europe

If you want to enjoy Japanese new sake in Europe, keep an eye out for arrivals from winter through early spring. Look for the words "shinshu" (new sake), "shiboritate" (freshly pressed) and "namazake" (sake that has not been pasteurised) on labels and product descriptions. Shipping from Japan takes time, so there is a lag between the release in Japan and the sake appearing in shops. Even so, namazake transported under refrigeration is more likely to keep its youthful character. After buying it, store it in the fridge and open it soon. Pouring it chilled into a white wine glass lets you appreciate how the aroma opens up, and even its subtle colour. It pairs easily with light seafood dishes and fresh cheese. Comparing it with an aged sake from the same brewery also lets you sense how sake changes with the seasons.
