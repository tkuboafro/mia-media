## Two yuzu-scented releases to celebrate 15 years

<figure class="embed embed--social"><blockquote class="twitter-tweet"><a href="https://x.com/faryeastbrewing/status/2122968299952828608">https://x.com/faryeastbrewing/status/2122968299952828608</a></blockquote></figure>

Japanese craft brewery Far Yeast Brewing has marked its 15th anniversary with two limited-edition releases: “馨和 KAGUA IPA” and “Azeotrope KAGUA GIN”. Both are built around the aroma of yuzu, a Japanese citrus fruit. Yuzu is small and yellow, with a bumpy rind. Its peel is prized more highly than its juice, and its aroma is showy and faintly bitter, something between lemon and mandarin. Part of the fun of this anniversary release is that two different drinks, a beer and a gin, are linked by the same fragrance.

## A European style sent from the Far East to the world

Far Yeast holds to the idea of taking beer born in Europe and delivering it to the world from Japan, in the “Far East”. IPA (India Pale Ale) is a style born in Britain in the 18th and 19th centuries, with a strong emphasis on hop aroma and bitterness. Gin, too, is a spirit with European roots: it spread through Britain from its origins in Dutch genever. The idea is to take traditional European styles, layer Japanese aromas over them, remake them and send them out into the world once more. These two releases clearly illustrate a trend that Japanese craft brewers have shown in recent years.

## Tasting one aroma in beer and in gin

In the beer, the yuzu aroma appears alongside the bitterness from the hops and the sweetness of the malt. In the gin, distillation is thought to bring the outline of the aroma into sharper relief. Being able to compare how the same citrus aroma shows up in a brewed drink and in a distilled one is what makes this pair special.

If you are looking for them in Europe, the quickest route is to ask specialist shops and importers that handle Japanese craft beer and Japanese gin whether they plan to stock them. As these are limited editions, quantities are likely to be small even if they do arrive. If you get hold of them, start by tasting the IPA alongside a British or Belgian IPA you know well, and see how the yuzu aroma works. Try the gin neat first to appreciate its aroma, then mix it with tonic and add a twist of citrus peel. Pair them with dishes that suit citrus, such as white-fish carpaccio or charcoal-grilled chicken, and you can enjoy at home the way yuzu is used and loved in Japan.
