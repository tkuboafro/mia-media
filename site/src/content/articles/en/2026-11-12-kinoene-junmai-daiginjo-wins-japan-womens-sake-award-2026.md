Kinoene Junmai Daiginjo Yamadanishiki 50 has won the top prize, "Bishu of the Year 2026", at the Japan Women's SAKE Award (Bishu Konkuru) 2026, a sake competition judged solely by women. It was brewed by Iinuma Honke, a sake brewery in Chiba Prefecture, just east of Tokyo. This year's competition drew 312 entries from Japan and abroad. The sake also received "TOP OF THE BEST", the top prize in the Fruity category.

## A competition that judges by taste, not by grade

Now in its fourth year, the competition was held this time in Sendai, Miyagi Prefecture, in north-eastern Japan. At the judging session on 6 September, around 180 women from six countries tasted the sake without knowing the brands. There were two types of judge: "regular judges", who hold a qualification in alcoholic beverages, and "amateur judges", who simply love sake. Sake has traditional grades determined by factors such as the degree of rice polishing, but this competition does not use them. Instead, entries are judged in six styles: Sparkling, Fruity, Light & Dry, Rich & Umami, Aged and Low Alcohol. Scoring has two parts: a 20-point scale for quality and a 50-point scale for how well the sake expresses its style. The top third of each category, 112 entries in total, won awards.

## The champion Kinoene and the winners of each category

Junmai daiginjo is one of the highest classes of sake. It is made without added brewing alcohol, using rice polished down to less than half its original size. Yamadanishiki is a rice variety especially prized in sake brewing. The top winners in the other categories were as follows. In Light & Dry, Hatsukame Junmai Daiginjo Hyogetsu; in Rich & Umami, Omekashi Kimoto Kijoshu; in Aged, Born Tenshi no Mezame; in Sparkling, Semizo CICADA Sparkling; and in Low Alcohol, Yukihikosan Wine Yeast Sake Yamadanishiki Sweet. The award chosen by the amateur judges went to Asabiraki's "Asabiraki BLACK STYLE ~Kuro-koji Jikomi~".

With this list of winners, you can choose sake by the flavour you like, without needing to know the names of the grades. If you are used to drinking wine, start by trying the Fruity category's Kinoene in a wine glass and see how its aroma unfolds. Then compare it with the winners of Light & Dry and Rich & Umami to get a sense of your own preferences. At importers and online shops, searching for the romanised "Kinoene" or "Iinuma Honke" should make it easier to find. The next competition is due to take place in Hiroshima Prefecture in 2027, with judging planned for early September.
