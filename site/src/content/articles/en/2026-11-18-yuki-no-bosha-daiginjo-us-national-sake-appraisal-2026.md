At the U.S. National Sake Appraisal held in Honolulu, Hawaii, sake from breweries in Akita Prefecture, on the Sea of Japan coast in north-eastern Japan, earned high praise. Six sakes from Akita received gold medals. Among them, Yuki no Bosha (Yuki no Bosha Daiginjo), brewed by Saiya Shuzoten in Yurihonjo, was named Runner-up Grand Prix in the Daiginjo B category, which is equivalent to third place.

## Third place in the Daiginjo B category

Daiginjo is one of the highest grades of sake. It is brewed with rice that has been polished down so that 50% or less of its original weight remains. This proportion is called the "seimai buai" (rice polishing ratio), and the more the brewer uses only the parts close to the heart of the grain, the more fragrant and the less coarse in flavour the sake tends to be. This year's Daiginjo B category covers sakes with a polishing ratio of 50% or less. This means the sake placed among the top three bottles in this category at a large-scale sake competition held in the United States.

## Six golds from seven entries

Saiya Shuzoten entered seven sakes this time, and six of them won gold medals. The Runner-up Grand Prix follows a win in the previous year and is the brewery's fifth in total. In October 2026, the same brewery also took first place in all three categories at the Akita Prefecture Sake Competition. It can be said to be a brewery that keeps earning recognition in both domestic and overseas judging.

Daiginjo is often thought of as a special glass to be enjoyed for its aroma. According to the brewery, however, Yuki no Bosha Daiginjo can also be enjoyed as a sake to drink with a meal. It is said to go particularly well with kiritanpo, a local dish of Akita. Kiritanpo is a hot pot dish in which mashed rice is wrapped around a stick and grilled, then simmered with vegetables and mushrooms in chicken stock.

If you are looking for this sake in Europe, check for the name "Yuki no Bosha" in the lists of specialist sake shops and importers. Even if you cannot get kiritanpo, you can try a similar pairing with warming dishes made with chicken soup, root vegetables and mushrooms. Drinking it slightly chilled in a wine glass makes it easier to appreciate both the aroma and how well it goes with the food. Competition results are a good opportunity to discover breweries you do not yet know.
