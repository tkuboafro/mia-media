The awards ceremony of the National Umeshu Competition 2026, which judges the quality of Japanese umeshu, was held on 20 November 2026, and the prizes were announced. Oimatsu Shuzo, based in Hita, Oita Prefecture, on the island of Kyushu, said on its official X account that its Tenku no Tsuki Kabosu Umeshu had won gold in the citrus blend umeshu category. The news shows that, in the world of umeshu, the style of combining ume with citrus is recognised as a category in its own right.

## News of the gold medal from the awards venue

<figure class="embed embed--social"><blockquote class="twitter-tweet"><a href="https://x.com/oimatsushuzo/status/2123687792400052476">https://x.com/oimatsushuzo/status/2123687792400052476</a></blockquote></figure>

Umeshu is a traditional Japanese liqueur made by steeping green ume plums in alcohol and sugar. With its good balance of sweetness and acidity, it is widely enjoyed in Japan as an aperitif or an after-dinner drink. The National Umeshu Competition gathers umeshu from all over Japan for judging, with categories divided according to the character of their aroma and flavour. According to Oimatsu Shuzo, the news of the award came from a member of staff who attended the ceremony. The winning entry was in the citrus blend umeshu category, which features umeshu that combine ume with citrus.

## Kabosu, and a brewery founded in 1789

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/40000143/pexels-photo-40000143.jpeg?auto=compress&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;dpr=2&amp;h=650&amp;w=940" alt="Kabosu, a strongly scented citrus fruit with green skin" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Kabosu, a strongly scented citrus fruit with green skin — Photo: Jahra Tasfia Reza (Pexels)</figcaption></figure>

Kabosu is a citrus fruit that represents Oita Prefecture. A small green fruit resembling a lime, it has a refreshing aroma and strong acidity, and in Japan its juice is often squeezed over dishes as a finishing touch. Combining this citrus with umeshu adds a sharp fragrance to the sweet-and-sour character of the ume. The producer, Oimatsu Shuzo, is a historic brewery founded in Hita in the first year of the Kansei era (1789). It also makes the sake Sanzui and the barley shochu Enma, among others, producing a wide range of drinks including sake, shochu and liqueurs.

## How to enjoy it in Europe

When looking for umeshu in Europe, it is worth checking the ranges of specialist shops and importers that handle sake and Japanese liqueurs for products labelled "umeshu". A citrus blend umeshu like this one is best tried first on the rocks, over ice, to appreciate how the aromas of ume and citrus layer together. Topped up with soda, it makes a light aperitif, and it also pairs easily with dishes that call for a squeeze of lemon, such as marinated seafood or grilled chicken. You might also look at the label for the word "kabosu" and the place of origin, to see for yourself the style that was recognised at the competition.
