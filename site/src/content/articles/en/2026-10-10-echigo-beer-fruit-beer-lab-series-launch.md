Echigo Beer, based in Niigata Prefecture on the Sea of Japan coast, has launched a new fruit beer series called "Fruit Beer Laboratory". The series has a look and a name distinct from the brewery's established flagship brand, and the first release went on sale on 16 October 2026. The aim is to draw people who do not usually drink beer into craft beer.

## A separate brand born from event-only beers

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://prcdn.freetls.fastly.net/release_image/176258/4/176258-4-d258147ddc31f4c834a47b2a1ea45274-691x363.png?width=1200&amp;height=900" alt="&quot;Fruit Beer Laboratory&quot;, launched as a brand separate from the existing Echigo Beer products" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>&quot;Fruit Beer Laboratory&quot;, launched as a brand separate from the existing Echigo Beer products — Image: PR TIMES（エチゴビール） (press release)</figcaption></figure>

Echigo Beer is a long-established brewery that calls itself Japan's "first craft beer" (jibiru). Jibiru is the term for the small breweries that sprang up across Japan after deregulation in the 1990s. The company had been serving limited-edition fruit beers at beer events, where they went down well and many people asked for them to be made into a product. So the brewery chose to move deliberately away from the traditional design built around its goat mark. The series is framed as a "laboratory" and has a bright, pop look. The cans carry a four-panel comic featuring three characters: Aroma, Hop and Dr. Goat. A dedicated social media account for the series will post in tandem with the comic.

## Pear and berries: two beers with no added flavourings

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://prcdn.freetls.fastly.net/release_image/176258/4/176258-4-088de1da74fbb775e34a94ef8aa86247-466x445.png?width=1200&amp;height=900" alt="Product image of the &quot;Fruit Beer Laboratory&quot; series" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Product image of the &quot;Fruit Beer Laboratory&quot; series — Image: PR TIMES（エチゴビール） (press release)</figcaption></figure>

The first release, "Le Lectier × Wheat Ale", uses juice from Le Lectier pears grown in Niigata Prefecture. It is a wheat-based ale that is lightly sweet, smooth on the palate and clean on the finish. The second release, "Double Berry × Red Ale", follows on 20 November. It combines strawberry juice from Tochigi Prefecture with raspberry juice and adds the toasty character of caramel malt. Poured into a glass, it turns a ruby colour. Both beers are 5% ABV and are sold in 350 ml cans. No flavourings are used, so the natural aroma of the fruit comes through.

## Why "happoshu" rather than "beer"?

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://prcdn.freetls.fastly.net/release_image/176258/4/176258-4-921d0371d64d14ef003c59ca50a6ce5e-465x457.png?width=1200&amp;height=900" alt="Cans of &quot;Fruit Beer Laboratory&quot; in a pop design" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Cans of &quot;Fruit Beer Laboratory&quot; in a pop design — Image: PR TIMES（エチゴビール） (press release)</figcaption></figure>

Under Japan's liquor tax law, these two beers fall into the category known as "happoshu" (sparkling alcoholic drinks). To be called "beer" in Japan, a product must contain at least 50% malt, and adjuncts other than malt must not exceed 5% of the malt's weight. This series meets the malt requirement, but because it uses 5% or more fruit-derived ingredients, it falls outside the beer category. Happoshu in Japan tends to suggest a cheap, low-malt beer, but here the category is simply the result of using plenty of fruit. The company plans to establish the two beers as permanent products and to release a third in about a year.

No sales in Europe have been announced for now. Even so, for readers familiar with Belgian fruit beers such as kriek and framboise, this is worth watching as an attempt to apply the same idea to Japanese fruit. Le Lectier in particular is not a fruit that is easy to come by in Europe, so the beer offers a good chance to get to know its aroma. If you do get hold of a bottle, it is worth pairing it with food or dessert, as the brewery suggests, to see how the flavour has been designed to stay away from cloying sweetness. Information on new products will be released gradually on the "fruitbeerken" accounts on X and Instagram.
