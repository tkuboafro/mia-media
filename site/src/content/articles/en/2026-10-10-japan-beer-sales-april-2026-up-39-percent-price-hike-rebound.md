On 15 May 2026, Nihon Keizai Shimbun (Nikkei) reported April sales results for the four major brewers: Asahi Breweries, Kirin Brewery, Suntory and Sapporo Breweries. Combined beer-category sales volume rose 39% year on year, the first increase in six months. However, the figure reflects how sharply sales fell in April last year rather than a genuine return of market momentum.

## April's figures by category and sales channel

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/19106394/pexels-photo-19106394.jpeg?auto=compress&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;dpr=2&amp;h=650&amp;w=940" alt="Beer being poured into a glass" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Beer being poured into a glass — Photo: Andrew Patrick Photo (Pexels)</figcaption></figure>

In Japan, the "beer category" is divided into three groups under the liquor tax system: beer, which uses a high proportion of malt; happoshu, which uses less malt; and the cheaper "third-category beer", made with ingredients other than malt. In April, beer rose 31%, topping the previous year for the first time in six months. The lower-priced segment of happoshu and third-category beer combined rose 53%, and third-category beer alone rose 69%, its first increase in 13 months. By sales channel, products for the home, such as cans, rose 42%, while products for restaurants and bars, such as bottles and kegs, rose 29%. Temperatures that were higher than usual across the country also helped.

## All four companies above last year, but flagship brands diverge

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/5858056/pexels-photo-5858056.jpeg?auto=compress&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;dpr=2&amp;h=650&amp;w=940" alt="Draught beers lined up on a counter" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Draught beers lined up on a counter — Photo: Pavel Danilyuk (Pexels)</figcaption></figure>

All four companies exceeded the same month last year. Suntory rose 47%, Sapporo Breweries 39% and Asahi Breweries 43%. Kirin Brewery, which reports on a value basis, rose 31%. Asahi resumed shipments of all products on 7 April; these had been halted after a system failure at its parent company in autumn 2025. By brand, Asahi "Super Dry" rose 47%, Sapporo "Black Label" 45% and Suntory "The Premium Malt's" 17%. Kirin "Ichiban Shibori", by contrast, fell 1% because of the rebound from a new product launched the previous year.

## Challenges that remain after a temporary boost

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/37919989/pexels-photo-37919989.jpeg?auto=compress&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;dpr=2&amp;h=650&amp;w=940" alt="Beer paired with small dishes" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Beer paired with small dishes — Photo: Iban Lopez Luna (Pexels)</figcaption></figure>

In March, sales fell by around 30% as stockpiling ahead of last year's price rises was reversed. Taken together, March and April were down 6% on the same period last year. Industry insiders see April's growth as the result of temporary factors. With prices rising, they say, consumer sentiment has cooled and beer-category products, being a discretionary treat, have slipped down the shopping list.

For European readers, the key point is how price-sensitive Japanese consumers are. The movement in the lower-priced segment mirrors the state of Japanese household budgets. Month-to-month swings are heavily influenced by special circumstances in the previous year, so looking across several months gives a truer picture. Tasting a dry lager such as Super Dry alongside a beer with a stronger malt flavour, and pairing them with salty dishes such as yakitori and edamame, makes it easier to see what people in Japan look for in beer in everyday life.
