## A Year Without Brewing After the Toji Left

Oimatsu Shuzo is a sake brewery founded 258 years ago in Yamasaki, Shiso City, in the Harima region of western Hyogo Prefecture. In sake making, the person who leads the craftsmen on the brewery floor and determines the house flavour is called the "toji" (master brewer). Last year, Oimatsu Shuzo was unable to secure a toji and had no choice but to skip that year's brewing. For a brewery that has continued for more than two and a half centuries, a year in which not a single bottle could be made was probably a serious crisis. For small regional breweries, finding brewers is also a challenge facing the Japanese sake industry as a whole.

## The New President Recovers the "Rich, Mellow" Taste

After the pause, the brewery moved to a new structure, and Takayuki Maeno, aged 39, became president. The first sake brewed under the new structure has gone on sale as a new sake. "Shinshu" (new sake) refers to young sake pressed in that winter, and it is the first indication of how the brewery's year has turned out. What Mr Maeno aimed for was not a new flavour, but to recreate the rich, mellow taste and aroma that Oimatsu Shuzo has long protected. Of the bottle, finished with the thought "I wanted to protect this taste", Mr Maeno says he feels a sense of achievement: "Somehow, we have managed to get this far." A photograph from the Kobe Shimbun shows Mr Maeno and Tomoya Morishita attaching labels, one by one, to bottles of the new sake.

## For European Readers: Focus on the "Inheritance of Flavour" in Long-Established Breweries

It is not known at present whether Oimatsu Shuzo's sake can be obtained in Europe. Even so, this story offers one way of looking at sake when choosing it. Whether a brewery can pass on its house flavour unchanged even when its brewers change is an important point in judging the sake of a long-established maker. Asking a sake retailer or importer about "sake from breweries that have changed hands" or "rich, full-bodied styles" may lead to new discoveries. Rich, mellow sake is said to open up its umami and aroma more readily when served at room temperature or slightly warmed rather than over-chilled. It is a sake that pairs easily with dishes of strong flavour, such as mature hard cheese or braised meat.
