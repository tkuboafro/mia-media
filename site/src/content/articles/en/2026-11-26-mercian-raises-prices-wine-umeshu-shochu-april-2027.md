## About 60 wine, plum wine and shochu products affected

Mercian is a drinks maker under Kirin Holdings. On 17 November 2026, it announced price rises for some of its wine, plum wine (umeshu) and shochu products, about 60 items in all. The new prices will apply to deliveries from 1 April 2027. Wine prices will rise by around 6–10%, while plum wine and shochu will go up by around 3–9%. The affected brands include Casillero del Diablo, a wine the company sells in Japan, Makkoi Umeshu, a plum wine, and Raku, a shochu.

## Rising costs of raw materials, logistics and packaging

As reasons for the increases, the company cites higher costs for raw materials, logistics and packaging. The affected products include both wines imported from overseas and umeshu and shochu, which are traditional Japanese drinks. Umeshu is a sweet and sour liqueur made by steeping unripe green ume fruit in a distilled spirit or similar base. Shochu is a Japanese distilled spirit made from ingredients such as rice, barley and sweet potato, and its aroma and flavour vary greatly depending on the ingredient. Because imported and domestic products are being repriced at the same time, the effect of rising costs appears to extend well beyond a handful of items and across the Japanese alcoholic drinks market as a whole.

## What this means from a European perspective

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/18443634/pexels-photo-18443634.jpeg?auto=compress&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;dpr=2&amp;h=650&amp;w=940" alt="Amber-coloured plum wine poured over ice in a glass" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Amber-coloured plum wine poured over ice in a glass — Photo: Markus Winkler (Pexels)</figcaption></figure>

The announcement concerns delivery prices within Japan, and no change to retail prices in Europe has been announced. However, higher costs for raw materials, logistics and packaging are a burden shared by producers who export drinks from Japan. It is a news item worth watching for anyone following price trends in Japanese drinks. When choosing umeshu or shochu in Europe, we recommend checking the ingredients and the alcohol content on the label. Umeshu is easy to pair as an aperitif or with desserts when served on the rocks or with soda. Shochu, served with cold water or hot water, makes the differences in character between ingredients easier to appreciate. If a particular brand catches your eye, it is worth asking the retailer or importer about availability and prices from next spring onwards.
