Naganuma, in Hokkaido, is an agricultural town close to Sapporo. The town's farming co-operative, JA Naganuma, has released a limited edition of "vegin tomato", a craft gin made with locally grown tomatoes. The project was a joint effort with the professional ice hockey team Red Eagles Hokkaido. The Nihon Nogyo Shimbun, a Japanese agricultural newspaper, reported the news on 6 October.

JA is the name for the agricultural co-operatives found across Japan. As well as collecting and shipping produce from farmers, they sell farming supplies and more. Gin is a spirit flavoured with a range of botanicals, centred on juniper berries. Using a vegetable such as the tomato rather than a fruit would be an unusual combination even for gin lovers in Europe. The fact that a co-operative is making a spirit from the main crop of its own area also reflects the current state of Japanese craft drinks.

## A limited edition overseen by an ice hockey player

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/6847602/pexels-photo-6847602.jpeg?auto=compress&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;dpr=2&amp;h=650&amp;w=940" alt="Image of an indoor rink where professional ice hockey matches are played" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Image of an indoor rink where professional ice hockey matches are played — Photo: Tima Miroshnichenko (Pexels)</figcaption></figure>

The full name of this release is "vegin tomato Red Eagles Hokkaido ver." The aroma, flavour and label design were overseen by team player Toi Kobayashi. It is said to be a special version that makes the most of the fresh flavour of the tomato, while keeping the drinking experience clean and giving the finish more depth. Each bottle holds 500 millilitres, and the price is an open price set by the retailer. Part of the proceeds will be donated to the team.

Last season, Red Eagles Hokkaido won two titles: the All Japan Championship and the Asia League. This season is the team's 100th anniversary, and they are aiming to defend both titles. JA Naganuma has been an official partner of the team since last season.

At present there is no information that this limited edition will be sold in Europe. Even so, across Japan craft gins are appearing that use local farm produce such as rice, citrus fruit and vegetables for flavouring. If you spot a Japanese gin in a specialist shop, try checking the ingredients and the producer named on the label. You should be able to see the link with the local farming. Some say that with a vegetable-based gin such as one made with tomatoes, mixing with tonic makes the aroma easier to appreciate. There are also examples of using it in a gin-based Bloody Mary, known as a "Red Snapper", and comparing how it pairs with the tomato it is made from is another way to enjoy it.
