## More than 80 alcohol companies at the "JAPAN MALL" in Shanghai

The China International Import Expo (CIIE) is a large-scale trade fair devoted to goods imported into China, held in Shanghai every year. At this year's 9th edition, the Japan External Trade Organization (JETRO), the Japanese government's trade promotion agency, brought together more than 150 Japanese food and drink companies to exhibit as a group. In the agricultural produce and food area, a 450-square-metre Japan pavilion, "JAPAN MALL", was set up, with the theme of delivering moments that satisfy the heart through food.

The alcohol section brought together more than 80 sake breweries and alcohol sales companies, displaying more than 190 products, including sake, umeshu (a sweet and sour fruit liqueur made by steeping green ume plums in alcohol and sugar), liqueurs and Japanese wine. The venue also featured a corner where professional bartenders made signature cocktails on the spot. In the food section, more than 60 companies exhibited, with more than 170 products on show, including seasonings, rice and noodles, confectionery, drinks and health foods.

## The keyword is "emotional consumption"

According to Tanaka Masayoshi, head of the E-commerce and Market Development Department at the JETRO Shanghai office, "emotional consumption", the pursuit of emotional satisfaction and comfort in everyday life, such as at mealtimes, is spreading in China. A growing number of Japanese companies are placing weight on this trend, and he says it was made one of the focal points of this year's exhibition. The cocktail demonstrations can be read as an effort to present Japanese alcohol not only in its traditional serving styles but also as an enjoyable experience.

Companies from the restaurant sector that already have outlets in some Chinese cities, such as the ramen chain Ippudo, also took part, aiming to use the expo as a stepping stone for further expansion. With food and restaurant companies exhibiting alongside the breweries, food and alcohol were presented together.

## Points of interest for European readers

The fact that the Japanese alcohol industry has begun to put "how people can enjoy it" at the forefront as it targets one of Asia's largest markets also offers hints for thinking about how to enjoy these drinks in Europe. Umeshu can be mixed with ice or soda and enjoyed as an aperitif. If you use sake in cocktails, the crisp style of junmai sake, made only from rice, rice koji and water, is said to be easy to work with, and it should pair well with citrus and sparkling water. When browsing importers' and Japanese restaurants' lists, looking beyond sake to umeshu, liqueurs and Japanese wine will reveal the breadth of Japanese alcohol.
