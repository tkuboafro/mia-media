The major property developer Mori Trust will hold the sake event "YORIMICHI SAKE KAIDO in Marunouchi" at Marunouchi Trust City, right next to Tokyo Station, on 3 and 4 December 2026. True to its name ("yorimichi" means a detour), it is an event where people can drop in casually on their way home from work and compare sakes from different regions. It is the final instalment of a series that has run through 2026 in four areas of central Tokyo. For European readers, what matters is not so much the event itself as the brands being presented here: sakes that have earned recognition in France are on the list.

## Two junmai daiginjo praised in Paris

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://prcdn.freetls.fastly.net/release_image/18049/342/18049-342-b762a50437d75efcbfb45f5647508720-500x500.png?width=1200&amp;height=900" alt="Bottle of an award-winning sake due to be served at the event" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Bottle of an award-winning sake due to be served at the event — Image: PR TIMES (press release)</figcaption></figure>

Of the more than 15 sakes on offer, two are the headline acts. Both have won gold several times at Kura Master, the sake competition held in Paris, France. The first is "Saika Junmai Daiginjo Yamadanishiki" from Wakayama Prefecture. Yamadanishiki is the premium sake rice variety most widely used in brewing. The second is "Shinsei Kyoto-san Iwai Junmai Daiginjo" from Kyoto Prefecture, made with the Kyoto sake rice "Iwai". Junmai daiginjo is the top category of premium designated sake: it is brewed without added brewer's alcohol, and the rice is milled away by at least half from the outside. As a result, it tends to have a gorgeous aroma and a light, smooth feel on the palate.

## Sakes from Wakayama, Kyoto and Ehime

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://prcdn.freetls.fastly.net/release_image/18049/342/18049-342-29c0a7570c188c231d712e39d278d24e-195x555.jpg?width=1200&amp;height=900" alt="Bottle of a regional sake due to be served at the venue" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Bottle of a regional sake due to be served at the venue — Image: PR TIMES (press release)</figcaption></figure>

Sakes from other regions will also be on offer. From Wakayama Prefecture come "Chokarakuchi Junmai-shu Minakata" and "Junmai Ginjo Kuroushi"; from Kyoto Prefecture, "Eikun Koto Sennen Junmai Daiginjo"; and from Ehime Prefecture on Shikoku, "Shirokawago Junmai Daiginjo". The line-up lets visitors compare a wide range of styles in one place, from a very dry junmai sake to an aromatic junmai daiginjo. All were chosen from regions with which Mori Trust has built ties through "CoCo JAPAN", its project run in partnership with local communities.

## Why a property company is introducing sake

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://prcdn.freetls.fastly.net/release_image/18049/342/18049-342-82fa3b0381e704dea6b56d2fe61b35e5-3900x2194.jpg?format=jpeg&amp;auto=webp&amp;fit=bounds&amp;width=2400&amp;height=1260" alt="Key visual for &quot;YORIMICHI SAKE KAIDO in Marunouchi&quot;" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Key visual for &quot;YORIMICHI SAKE KAIDO in Marunouchi&quot; — Image: PR TIMES (press release)</figcaption></figure>

Next to the venue are "TIC TOKYO", a tourist information centre certified by the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO), and the bar "BAR of TOKYO", and the event is being held in cooperation with both. A major urban developer using a single glass of sake to spark wider interest in regional climate and culture is one example of how collaboration between companies and regions is spreading in Japan's sake industry.

If you want to enjoy such sakes in Europe, a good first step is to use the Kura Master list of award winners as a guide. Sakes chosen by French judges tend to suit the tastes of people used to wine. Try asking importers and wine shops for "Saika" and "Shinsei". A junmai daiginjo is best served slightly chilled in a white-wine glass, which brings out its aroma. A dry junmai sake pairs easily with seafood dishes and salty cheeses.
