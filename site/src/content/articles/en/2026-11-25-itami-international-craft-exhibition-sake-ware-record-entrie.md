The taste of sake is not decided by the sake alone. What vessel it is served in, and how it is presented, also matter. In an international open call themed on precisely that vessel, entries from overseas have reached their highest number yet.

## A sake town opens a call for entries for sake vessels

Itami is a town between Osaka and Kobe that has prospered on sake brewing since the Edo period. The Itami International Craft Exhibition, organised by Itami Museum, the city's cultural facility, invited entries in 2026 on the theme of "sake vessels and sakebai-dai" (cup stands). A sakebai-dai is a stand on which a sake cup is placed to be presented. This is the 12th time the exhibition has been held on this theme. The awards ceremony took place on 14 November at the former Okada family residence and sake brewery, which remain on the museum grounds, where certificates were presented to the winners of the Grand Prize, the Semi-Grand Prize, the Award of Excellence and others.

## Overseas entries exceed one third of the total

This year, 91 entries came from 12 countries and regions overseas, a record. That figure is more than one third of all entries. The Grand Prize went to a work by Cho Hyeon-yeong. The Semi-Grand Prize went to a work by Kim Yun-ji, and the prize is called the "Hakuyuki Itami Morohaku Prize". Hakuyuki is the name of an Itami sake brand. Morohaku is a brewing method in which polished white rice is used both for the rice for the koji and for the rice added to the moromi (fermenting mash). Itami was once known as a producer of this clear sake. A prize that carries on the history of the sake town has thus been awarded to a maker from overseas.

In 2024, traditional Japanese sake brewing was inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list. Alongside brewing techniques, international interest is also spreading to the tools used to enjoy sake. This result can be seen as a sign of that. If you are enjoying sake in Europe, start by tasting the sake you have on hand in different vessels. A wide, flat cup is said to release aroma easily, while a glass with a narrowed rim is said to keep the aroma concentrated, so the same sake can leave a different impression. You may also find vessels that suit sake well among the work of local potters and glass artists. The Itami International Craft Exhibition accepts entries from overseas, so following future calls for entries as a maker is another way to enjoy it.
