At Japanese breweries, the first sake of the year is shipped out one batch after another from autumn into winter. Mie Prefecture lies south-west of Nagoya, on the Pacific side. In Yokkaichi, an industrial city in the north of the prefecture, Miyazaki Honten will put on sale on 25 November "Miya no Yuki Junmai Shiboritate Genshu", the first sake it brewed this season. According to the brewers, this year's rice was easy to draw umami from.

## What "Shiboritate" and "Genshu" Mean

Each word in the name says something about the character of the sake. "Junmai" means a sake made only from rice, rice koji and water, with no added brewer's alcohol. "Shiboritate" refers to new sake that is shipped straight after the fermented mash has been pressed, and its hallmark is a young, lively aroma. "Genshu" means the step of adding water after pressing to adjust the strength has been left out, so the flavour tends to be richer and the alcohol content somewhat higher. Put together, the three terms describe a spirited bottle that can only be enjoyed at the start of the season.

## A Crisp Taste from Gohyakumangoku

The main ingredient is Gohyakumangoku, a variety grown for sake brewing. This rice is known for tending to produce a light sake with a clean finish. According to Koji Asada of the company's manufacturing section, the result has a crisp, clean taste. On top of that, it was a year in which umami was easier to draw out than usual. This looks set to be a new sake that carries the taste of rice within its lightness.

Production is limited. There are 4,440 bottles of 720 ml (1,760 yen) and 2,620 bottles of 300 ml (748 yen), and sales will centre on Mie Prefecture.

## Enjoying the New Sake Season in Europe

This brand is a limited release for the local market, so there is little chance of coming across the same bottle in Europe. Even so, the shiboritate style itself can be enjoyed every winter. Shops that stock sake sometimes display new sake labelled "Shiboritate", "Nama" or "Genshu" from November into the beginning of the following year. Once you have bought a bottle, we recommend chilling it well and drinking it from a wine glass, in which the aroma opens up. Genshu has a higher alcohol content, so it is best sipped slowly. It pairs easily with light-flavoured dishes such as sashimi, white fish carpaccio and salted yakitori. As with Beaujolais Nouveau, why not choose it as a seasonal pleasure, a way of discovering how the year has turned out?
