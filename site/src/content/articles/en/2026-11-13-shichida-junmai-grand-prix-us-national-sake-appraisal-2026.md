Shichida Junmai, a junmai sake from Tenzan Shuzo in Ogi, Saga Prefecture, in north-western Kyushu, won the top prize, the Grand Prix, at the U.S. National Sake Appraisal held in Hawaii in October 2026. It won in "Junmai Division B", which judges junmai sake with a polishing ratio above 60%. This is the first time the brewery has received the Grand Prix. The winner was not a limited edition but the brewery's standard bottling, which it makes all year round.

## A competition with a long history outside Japan

The U.S. National Sake Appraisal has been held every year since 2001 and is one of the longest-running sake competitions outside Japan. This year 486 entries from 153 breweries were submitted, and blind judging, with the labels hidden, took place across five divisions. Junmai sake is sake made only from rice, rice koji and water, with no added brewer's alcohol. The polishing ratio shows the proportion of rice remaining after milling. The higher the number, the less of the grain has been removed and the more of the rice's flavour remains. Junmai Division B had 50 entries, and 14 were first selected for gold medals. Shichida Junmai was then chosen as the Grand Prix winner from among them.

Fifteen entries came from Saga Prefecture, of which three won gold and four won silver. Tenzan Shuzo also won a gold medal for its Shichida Junmai Daiginjo. In addition, BOOST 2026 from Mitsutake Shuzojo took gold in Junmai Division B. Kensuke Shichida, the company president, also chairs the Saga Prefecture Sake Brewers' Association, and he sees the result as recognition of the quality of Saga's sake as a whole.

## A food-friendly sake that brings out the umami of rice

Shichida Junmai is characterised by the clear umami and sweetness of the rice itself. It is smooth to drink both chilled and warm, and the judges praised it highly as a food-friendly sake that brings out the best in a meal. It is brewed with Reiho, a rice grown in Saga Prefecture, and Yamada Nishiki, a rice known as well suited to sake brewing. Over the past two years, rice prices in Japan have swung sharply. President Shichida expressed his gratitude to the farmers who have kept growing rice regardless. He hopes the award will raise the brewery's profile at home and abroad and help widen its market.

## How to enjoy it in Europe

If you see the name "Shichida" at a sake specialist or restaurant, that is this brewery's sake. Shichida Junmai is a sake whose flavour changes with temperature. Try it chilled first, then lightly warmed to around 40°C, and compare how the sweetness and umami come through. It also goes well with everyday dishes rich in umami, such as roast chicken, mushroom dishes and mature cheese. This award also shows that it is not only expensive sake made from heavily polished rice that gets recognised. Looking at the polishing ratio on the label and choosing a junmai sake that has not been polished very much could be a new way to enjoy sake.
