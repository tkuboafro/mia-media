Inoue Umeboshi Shokuhin Co., Ltd. makes umeboshi (pickled plums) in Minabe, Wakayama Prefecture, on the Kii Peninsula south of Osaka. Its plum liqueur, Kumahei, has won two awards at once at the San Francisco World Spirits Competition 2026 (SFWSC) in the United States. One is the top prize in the fruit liqueur class; the other is Best in Show Liqueur, the highest honour among all liqueurs. Umeshu is a traditional Japanese liqueur made by steeping ume (a Japanese fruit also known in English as the Japanese apricot) in alcohol together with rock sugar.

## One bottle chosen from more than 4,000 entries

SFWSC began in 2000. It has the longest history of any spirits competition in North America and is among the largest in the world. Bartenders, distillers and journalists judge more than 4,000 entries in blind tastings, with the brands concealed. Only spirits that every judge rates as gold, known as Double Gold, go through to the final round. From there, a Best of Class is chosen for each class, and one bottle per category is then named Best in Show. The results were announced at the awards ceremony on 8 November 2026 (local time). In their comments, the judges praised the liqueur as concentrated yet well balanced, with acidity and fruit in good harmony, and noted a depth reminiscent of dried fruit and an impression of an orchard.

## Large Nanko-ume plums and just three ingredients

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://prcdn.freetls.fastly.net/release_image/38738/8/38738-8-44258a1ebcb8547faf63760b522bed2e-3900x2600.jpg?width=1200&amp;height=900" alt="Kumahei plum liqueur by Inoue Umeboshi Shokuhin" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Kumahei plum liqueur by Inoue Umeboshi Shokuhin — Image: PR TIMES (press release)</figcaption></figure>

Kumahei is made with Nanko-ume, Wakayama's best-known plum variety, which has thin skin, thick flesh and a showy aroma. The company uses only plums grown on its own farms, and only large fruit of size 3L or above. The amount of plums is said to be far greater than in a typical umeshu. What the company cares about most is watching the plums change from green fruit to full ripeness and steeping them when their aroma is at its best. The only ingredients are plums, rock sugar and brewer's alcohol, and no other flavourings are added, the company says. The liqueur is 16% ABV and comes in a 720 ml bottle, with a suggested retail price in Japan of 3,300 yen including tax. Representative director Kazuo Hanamura says the award was won together with the growers who have protected Nanko-ume and with customers.

## How to enjoy it in Europe

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/2763380/pexels-photo-2763380.jpeg?auto=compress&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;dpr=2&amp;h=650&amp;w=940" alt="Umeshu served in a glass with a large ice cube" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Umeshu served in a glass with a large ice cube — Photo: Markus Winkler (Pexels)</figcaption></figure>

Kumahei is currently sold mainly through the company's online shop and a few shops in Japan, and it is not yet clear whether it will reach Europe. The award is likely to draw attention to it, though. When choosing an umeshu that is available to you, the ingredient list on the label is a useful guide. One made only from plums, sugar and alcohol should let the natural aroma of the fruit come through. An umeshu of around 16% ABV can be served on the rocks over a large ice cube, so you can enjoy how the aroma changes as it warms. It could also work as an after-dinner drink with blue cheese, nuts or dark chocolate.
