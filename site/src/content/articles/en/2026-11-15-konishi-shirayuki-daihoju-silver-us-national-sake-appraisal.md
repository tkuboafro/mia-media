On 10 November, Konishi Brewing, a sake brewery in Itami, Hyogo Prefecture, announced that its “Shirayuki Junmai Daiginjo Daihoju” (大褒寿) had won silver at the 2026 U.S. National Sake Appraisal. It is one of the results the brewery has earned overseas, this time at a sake competition held in the United States.

The award was in the “Daiginjo A” category, which covers sake with a polishing ratio of 40% or less. The polishing ratio is the proportion of the rice that remains after the brown rice has been milled. At 40%, more than 60% of the outer part of the grain is milled away, and only the core is used for brewing. Removing the proteins and fats that are concentrated in the outer layers tends to produce a sake with fewer off-flavours and a fragrant aroma, but it takes more effort and costs more. “Junmai daiginjo” refers to daiginjo made only from rice, rice koji and water, with no added brewer's alcohol, and it sits at the top of the sake grades.

## Yamada Nishiki, Itami water and the skill of the Tamba toji

The rice used is Yamada Nishiki from Hyogo Prefecture, regarded as the finest sake rice. The brewing water is from Itami, known for its soft mouthfeel. Itami is a town between Osaka and Kobe that has long been a centre of sake brewing. The sake is made slowly, using techniques inherited from the Tamba toji, a guild of master brewers from the Tamba region of Hyogo Prefecture. It is a bottle that combines local rice, local water and the methods of local craftsmen. In response to the award, Konishi Brewing said it will work to improve its quality even further.

## How to enjoy it in Europe

Junmai daiginjo is one of the easiest categories to find among the ranges of sake specialist shops and importers in Europe. On labels and product descriptions, terms such as “Junmai Daiginjo”, “Yamada Nishiki” and “Hyogo” are useful clues. The aroma of this type of sake is delicate, so serving it lightly chilled at around 10°C in a tulip-shaped wine glass brings out its fragrance well. It pairs easily with dishes that let the ingredients speak for themselves, such as white fish carpaccio, scallops or mild cheeses. Keeping an eye on brands recognised at U.S. competitions can also give you a clue as to which sakes are being valued in markets outside Japan.
