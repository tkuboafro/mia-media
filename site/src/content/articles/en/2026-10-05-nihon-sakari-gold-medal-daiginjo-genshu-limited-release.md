## What "gold" means

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://prcdn.freetls.fastly.net/release_image/41514/110/41514-110-474a71e03bc843611dcefc943a17c497-1034x756.jpg?width=1200&amp;height=900" alt="Nihonsakari&#x27;s &quot;Gold Award Daiginjo Genshu&quot;, released in limited numbers" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Nihonsakari&#x27;s &quot;Gold Award Daiginjo Genshu&quot;, released in limited numbers — Image: PR TIMES（日本盛） (press release)</figcaption></figure>

The National New Sake Awards (Zenkoku Shinshu Kanpyokai) began in 1911 and is one of the longest-running sake competitions in Japan. It is co-hosted by the National Research Institute of Brewing, a state research body, and the Japan Sake and Shochu Makers Association, the industry group. Each year, several hundred new sakes from breweries across the country are entered, and gold awards go only to the most outstanding among the prizewinners. Nihonsakari Co., Ltd., headquartered in Nishinomiya, Hyogo Prefecture, won gold for the fourth year running with sake brewed in 2025 (the 7th year of Reiwa brewing year). It has put the winning sake on sale in limited numbers from 1 October 2026, in a 720ml bottle at 5,500 yen including tax.

## A genshu of Yamada Nishiki polished to 38%

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://prcdn.freetls.fastly.net/release_image/41514/110/41514-110-6ba449be9f6678252ef696ab7593b88c-282x1099.jpg?format=jpeg&amp;auto=webp&amp;fit=bounds&amp;width=2400&amp;height=1260" alt="Nihonsakari&#x27;s gold-winning Daiginjo Genshu, sold in a 720ml bottle" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Nihonsakari&#x27;s gold-winning Daiginjo Genshu, sold in a 720ml bottle — Image: PR TIMES（日本盛） (press release)</figcaption></figure>

The rice used is Yamada Nishiki grown only in Nishioku, Yokawa-cho, Hyogo Prefecture. Yamada Nishiki is the leading premium sake rice, and Yokawa-cho is rated as a "Special A district", the top tier among its growing areas. A polishing ratio of 38% means that the outer 62% of the brown rice has been milled away, leaving only the core. Daiginjo is one of the top grades, requiring the rice to be polished to 50% or less. Because it is a "genshu", with no water added at the end, the alcohol content is somewhat high at 16 to 17%. A small amount of brewer's alcohol has been added to bring out the aroma. The sake meter value is +2.0 and the acidity is 1.2.

## The toji's aim: a "restrained aroma"

<figure class="embed embed--image"><img src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1616240580835-85fec1c8188b?crop=entropy&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;fit=max&amp;fm=jpg&amp;ixid=M3wxMDg1MTk5fDB8MXxzZWFyY2h8Mnx8c2FrZSUyMHdpbmUlMjBnbGFzc3xlbnwxfDB8fHwxNzkxMjIwNDU5fDA&amp;ixlib=rb-4.1.0&amp;q=80&amp;w=1080" alt="An image of chilled daiginjo poured into a wine glass" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>An image of chilled daiginjo poured into a wine glass — Photo: Anna Kumpan (<a href="https://unsplash.com/?utm_source=the_sake_wire&amp;utm_medium=referral" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Unsplash</a>)</figcaption></figure>

According to Nagatoshi Nakane, the toji (master brewer) in charge of production, this year he managed fermentation to bring out the character of the yeast and koji, deliberately keeping the aroma more subdued than in previous years. He then finished the sake so that it goes down cleanly. As the brewery describes it, an apple-like aroma rises the moment it touches the mouth, followed by a banana-like aroma that spreads across the palate. The texture is smooth, with sweetness and acidity well balanced, giving a light, slightly sweet taste.

This limited release is sold through online shops and directly operated stores in Japan, so finding the same bottle in Europe will be difficult. Even so, on the shelves of imported sake, the words "gold award", "Yamada Nishiki" and "daiginjo genshu" are a useful guide to quality. Daiginjo is best served not too cold, in a white wine glass, which makes its fruity aromas easier to appreciate. Because genshu is relatively strong, pour small amounts at a time and pair it with sashimi, white fish or light cheeses.
