Shikoku, in the south-west of Japan, is one of the country's four main islands. Minakuchi Shuzo (Minakuchi Shuzo) of Matsuyama, Ehime Prefecture, on Shikoku, won Excellence Awards in all three categories (ginjo, chilled sake and warmed sake) at the 2026 Shikoku Sake Appraisal. It repeated its clean sweep of the previous year, and it is the only brewery in Ehime to have won every category two years in a row. What is more, the prizes came for different brands from last year. All three of this year's brands belong to the new NIKITATSU series, launched in February 2026.

## An appraisal judged at different temperatures

The Shikoku Sake Appraisal is a quality competition held by the Takamatsu Regional Taxation Bureau, a national tax authority, for sake producers in the four prefectures of Shikoku. This year 92 entries came in from 34 breweries, and each category awarded 12 Excellence Awards, 36 in total. The judging took place over two days in September, with a total of 23 brewing experts, researchers and others tasting the sake with the brewery names concealed. The temperature is worth noting: ginjo is tasted at about 20°C, chilled sake at about 10°C and warmed sake at about 45°C, and the harmony of aroma and flavour is assessed. According to the Takamatsu Regional Taxation Bureau, high temperatures while the rice was ripening made this year's rice hard and slow to dissolve. Even so, it said, improved techniques for handling hard rice meant that many fine sakes were entered.

## Three sakes from the new NIKITATSU series

Founded in 1895, Minakuchi Shuzo is the only sake brewery in Dogo, an area known for its hot springs. To mark its 130th anniversary, it reviewed the four main products of its flagship brand Nikitatsu (仁喜多津) and relaunched them as NIKITATSU. The winners were NIKITATSU Junmai Daiginjo in the ginjo category, NIKITATSU Junmai Ginjo in the chilled sake category and NIKITATSU Junmai in the warmed sake category. "Junmai" refers to sake made only from rice, rice koji and water. "Ginjo" and "Daiginjo" are higher grades made with rice milled more heavily, that is, with a lower seimaibuai (rice polishing ratio), which expresses how much of the outside of the rice is polished away. The brewery has adopted the slogan "From Dogo to the world, from the world to Dogo" and is building a brand with overseas markets in mind.

## How to enjoy it in Europe

What this award shows is that three sakes from a single brewery were highly rated chilled, at room temperature and warmed. When choosing sake in Europe, look for "Junmai Daiginjo", "Junmai Ginjo" and "Junmai" on the label. To come close to the conditions used in the appraisal, try the Junmai Daiginjo, which is best for enjoying aroma, in a wine glass at close to room temperature, the Junmai Ginjo chilled, and the Junmai warmed to around 40–45°C. In the colder months, you could also try how warmed sake pairs with stews and cheese.
